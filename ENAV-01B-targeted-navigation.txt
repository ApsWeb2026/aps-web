L88: ## What Does Biology Explain?
L89: 
L90: Biology explains many different phenomena. It investigates how genes are expressed, how molecular and physiological mechanisms operate, how organisms develop, how behaviour changes in relation to environmental conditions, how ecological interactions are organised, and how populations and lineages evolve. These explanations employ different methods and identify different dependencies because they address different explananda.
L91: 
L92: There is therefore no reason to assume that biological explanation must ultimately reduce to a single explanatory question. A mechanistic explanation of a molecular pathway, an evolutionary explanation of a trait distribution, and an ecological explanation of population dynamics can each succeed relative to their own targets without first being reconstructed as explanations of living organisation.
L93: 
L94: APS nevertheless asks a further question. Living systems are themselves distinctive forms of organisation whose continuity is maintained despite continual change. Their material constituents turn over, their physiological states fluctuate, development transforms them, their relations with their environments change, and their organisation can be disrupted and restored. How such systems maintain and re-establish the conditions of their continuity is therefore a biological problem requiring explanation.
L95: 
L96: APS calls this problem **organised persistence**. It proposes organised persistence as a principal explanatory target for investigating living organisation, not as the obligatory explanandum of biology as a whole.
L97: 
L98: For a guided entry into how APS develops biological explanation, see:

L102: ## Where This Article Fits
L103: 
L104: This article establishes the explanatory target that precedes the more detailed APS analysis of biological explanation. Its question is not how all biological explanations should be structured, but why the persistence of living organisation constitutes an explanatory problem in its own right.
L105: 
L106: The distinction matters because APS does not infer from the existence of living organisation that every biological explanandum must ultimately be redescribed in terms of organised persistence. Instead, it asks when continuity, viability, repair, regulation, transformation, or related features of living organisation are themselves at issue and therefore require organisational explanation.

L58: ## Introduction
L59: 
L60: A new explanation earns credit for what it adds relative to a strong account of the same targetÃ¢â‚¬â€not simply for being newer, broader or more integrated.
L61: 
L62: ## Gain Is Relational
L63: 
L64: Explanatory gain is not an intrinsic property that an explanation possesses in isolation. It is a relation among at least four things:
L65: 
L66: - a candidate explanation;
L67: - a specified explanatory target;
L68: - a sufficiently strong relevant comparator; and
L69: - the evidence available for assessing their difference.
L70: 
L71: This matters because a proposal may appear powerful when considered alone but add little once the strongest relevant explanation of the same target has been reconstructed. Conversely, an account that is narrow in scope may still add something important if it explains, discriminates, constrains or supports an inference that the comparator does not already provide.
L72: 
L73: The relevant question is therefore not simply whether the candidate is informative, coherent or scientifically interesting. It is whether some target-relevant explanatory capacity remains after the comparator has received full credit for what it already explains.
L74: 
L75: Ross (2025) emphasises the plurality of explanatory aims and forms found in biology. That plurality is compatible with disciplined comparative assessment, but it makes a relational conception of gain especially important. Different explanations may succeed in different respects, and no single property automatically determines whether one has gained over another.
L76: 
L77: ## Difference Is Not Gain
L78: 
L79: A fair comparison may reveal a genuine difference between candidate explanations. That difference is necessary for positive explanatory gain: if the candidate adds nothing distinguishable, there is nothing further to credit. But difference alone is not sufficient.
L80: 
L81: A difference may consist in terminology, conceptual organisation, descriptive grain, formal representation, emphasis, scope, or explanatory aim. It may clarify a problem without solving an additional part of it. It may re-express dependencies already captured by the comparator. It may connect previously separate bodies of work without establishing a new explanatory consequence.
L82: 
L83: The anti-redescription control is therefore straightforward:
L84: 
L85: > A candidate explanation does not gain merely by describing the same explanatory work in a new vocabulary.
L86: 
L87: A target-relevant difference becomes evidence of gain only when the difference supports additional explanatory capacity that survives comparison with the strongest relevant alternative.

L211: ## Explanatory Gain Is Bounded
L212: 
L213: Every gain verdict is bounded by the comparison that produced it.
L214: 
L215: At minimum, the conclusion should identify:
L216: 
L217: - the candidate explanation;
L218: - the explanatory target;
L219: - the comparator;
L220: - the evidence used to assess the difference; and
L221: - the respect in which additional capacity was or was not demonstrated.
L222: 
L223: A positive verdict therefore has the form: this account provides additional explanatory capacity for this target relative to this sufficiently strong comparator on this evidence.
L224: 
L225: That statement does not imply that the account is superior on every target. It does not imply that the comparator should be abandoned. It does not imply that all explanations associated with one framework are better than all explanations associated with another.
L226: 
L227: The same boundedness applies to null and unresolved outcomes. Failure to demonstrate gain on one target does not establish general equivalence or inferiority, while unresolved evidence does not become a permanent verdict about the framework as a whole.
L228: 
L229: This local character is essential if explanatory gain is to remain an evidential conclusion rather than a rhetorical label.
L230: 
L231: ## A Practical Test for Explanatory Gain
L232: 
L233: After fair comparison, the practical question is:
L234: 
L235: > **What does the candidate explanation enable us to explain, discriminate, constrain or infer that the strongest relevant comparator does not already enable us to do?**
L236: 
L237: The wording is deliberately plural. Additional explanatory capacity may appear in different forms depending on the target.
L238: 
L239: A disciplined assessment should therefore ask whether the candidate provides a residual contribution that survives reconstruction of the strongest comparator. If the answer is yes and the contribution is supported by evidence, a bounded positive-gain verdict is warranted. If no additional capacity is established, the appropriate result is no additional gain. If the evidence cannot yet decide the matter, the result is unresolved.
L240: 
L241: The test does not replace specialist scientific judgment. It organises that judgment around the specific comparative burden that must be met before a difference is credited as gain.
L242: 
L243: ## What This Article Establishes
L244: 
L245: This article establishes that explanatory gain is:
L246: 
L247: - **relational:** it is assessed against a sufficiently strong relevant comparator;
L248: - **target-relative:** it concerns a specified explanatory question rather than a theory in the abstract;
L249: - **evidence-dependent:** additional capacity must be demonstrated rather than presumed;
L250: - **plural in form:** gain may concern mechanism, development, constraint, dynamics, discrimination, robustness, prediction or other target-relevant explanatory capacities;
L251: - **compatible with complementarity:** gain need not imply replacement;
L252: - **distinct from usefulness:** scientific value may exist without demonstrated gain;
L253: - **distinct from novelty and formalisation:** newness or mathematical sophistication does not settle the comparative question; and
L254: - **bounded in outcome:** positive gain, no additional gain and unresolved assessment are all legitimate local conclusions.
L255: 
L256: The central requirement is residual explanatory capacity. A difference becomes gain only when something target-relevant remains that the strongest comparator does not already provide.
L257: 
L258: ## What This Article Does Not Establish
L259: 
L260: The account does not provide a universal numerical measure of explanatory quality. It does not require every explanatory gain to be mechanistic, predictive or formally expressible. It does not convert integration, unification, novelty, usefulness or formalisation into automatic evidence of superiority.
L261: 
L262: It also does not infer framework-wide victory from local explanatory success. A candidate may gain on one target while remaining complementary, weaker or simply different on another. No result established here determines in advance which contemporary biological framework is best.
L263: 
L264: The article therefore stops where the next question begins. Once explanatory gain has been defined and bounded, contemporary frameworks can be examined for the kinds of explanatory work they actually perform without assuming beforehand that one form of explanation must dominate the others.
