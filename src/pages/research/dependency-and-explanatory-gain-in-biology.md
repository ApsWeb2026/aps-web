## 1. Introduction: The Dependency Problem in Biological Explanation

Biological explanation is pervasively concerned with dependencies. Organisms develop, regulate, repair, reproduce, and respond to changing conditions through processes in which outcomes depend on the presence, absence, state, timing, or organisation of other processes and conditions. Yet the explanatory significance of such dependencies is not captured merely by observing that one biological outcome depends upon something else. Contemporary accounts of scientific explanation already provide sophisticated resources for specifying relations of causal production, mechanistic organisation, intervention-sensitive difference-making, counterfactual dependence, and the integration of heterogeneous explanatory resources. Mechanistic accounts, for example, explain phenomena through organised entities and activities and the relations through which they produce the phenomenon of interest (Machamer, Darden, and Craver 2000; Craver 2007; Darden 2006). Interventionist accounts characterise causal explanatory relevance through patterns of dependence revealed by appropriate interventions (Woodward 2003), while counterfactual approaches extend the explanatory importance of what-if-things-had-been-different reasoning beyond any simple identification of causal sequence (Reutlinger 2016). Difference-making has likewise been examined explicitly in relation to biological explanation (Waters 2007). Dependency, in these broad senses, is therefore already deeply embedded in explanatory practice.

This creates a problem for any proposal that seeks to make dependency more explicit in biological explanation. The problem is not to establish that biological systems contain dependencies. Nor is it sufficient to translate an existing account into statements of the form that one condition, activity, or outcome depends on another. If the relevant relations are already adequately represented by a mechanistic, causal, regulatory, developmental, or other established explanation, their reformulation in dependency language may change the description without improving the explanation. The methodological challenge is consequently discriminative: when does explicit dependency analysis reveal explanatory structure that is not yet adequately integrated, and when does it merely redescribe an explanation that already captures the relevant relations?

This problem is sharpened rather than diminished by the diversity of established approaches to explanation. Mechanistic explanation has demonstrated the importance of organised causal production in biology, while interventionist and counterfactual approaches have made difference-making relations explicit in ways that permit systematic comparison of actual and possible changes (Machamer, Darden, and Craver 2000; Woodward 2003; Reutlinger 2016). These traditions overlap without being reducible to one another. In biological contexts, moreover, the explanatory importance of actual difference-makers need not coincide straightforwardly with a single abstract account of causal relevance (Waters 2007). Critical examination of interventionism has similarly identified limits to treating intervention-sensitive dependencies as an exhaustive account of explanatory relevance (Saatsi and Pexton 2013), including in biological cases where mechanistic explanation contains explanatorily important features not captured by a particular interventionist reconstruction (Fagan 2016). The relevant lesson for present purposes is not that one of these approaches should replace the others. It is that dependencies already occur within multiple established explanatory frameworks, and their explanatory role must therefore be assessed relative to what those frameworks already accomplish.

This point also cautions against treating explanatory integration as requiring theoretical uniformity. Biological phenomena frequently admit explanations drawing on different kinds of relations, investigative practices, and explanatory resources. Integrative pluralism provides one way of understanding why such heterogeneity need not be regarded as a temporary defect awaiting reduction to a single privileged explanatory form (Mitchell 2002). For the present study, the consequence is methodological. A dependency-based analysis should not be judged successful merely because it can restate heterogeneous explanatory findings in a common vocabulary. Common representation may facilitate comparison, but comparative convenience and substantive explanatory gain are not the same achievement. A method intended to assess dependency must therefore be capable of recognising when its own application adds little or nothing to the explanation under examination.

The present study addresses this problem by developing a neutral Dependency-Hypothesis Audit. The audit is not proposed as a new theory of scientific explanation, nor as an alternative to mechanistic, interventionist, counterfactual, regulatory, developmental, or other established approaches. Its narrower purpose is comparative and diagnostic. It asks whether explicitly specifying the dependency structure relevant to a biological explanandum contributes explanatory integration beyond that already supplied by the strongest established account. Crucially, the method permits a negative answer. Successful representation of a phenomenon in dependency terms does not itself count as explanatory gain. A useful audit must be able to distinguish residual explanatory contribution from systematic redescription and, where appropriate, return a null result.

The governing question is therefore: Can explicit dependency analysis be operationalised as a neutral comparative method for determining when the dependency structure of an established biological explanation provides residual explanatory gain and when it amounts only to redescription? The central thesis advanced here is correspondingly bounded. Explicit dependency analysis can function as a comparative audit that distinguishes cases in which making dependency structure explicit provides residual explanatory integration from cases in which the same analysis merely redescribes relations already adequately represented by established biological explanation. This is an integrative-methodological claim. It neither requires dependency itself to be novel nor entails that dependency analysis enjoys explanatory priority over the approaches against which it is compared.

The claim is examined through three deliberately contrasting calibration cases. Planarian regeneration provides a positive/residual calibration: the question is whether explicit dependency analysis integrates relations among established explanatory resources in a way that leaves some identifiable explanatory gain. Pristionchus pacificus mouth-form polyphenism provides a transfer calibration, testing whether the unchanged method remains informative in a different developmental and regulatory setting while permitting little or no residual gain. Mammalian glucose homeostasis provides the null calibration, asking whether the audit recognises a case in which mature physiological explanation already represents the relevant dependencies sufficiently explicitly that dependency analysis contributes no substantive explanatory improvement. The cases are purposively selected for these calibration functions; they are not presented as a representative sample of biological explanation.

The resulting comparison is consequently designed to test discrimination rather than ubiquity. A method that identifies explanatory gain wherever it identifies dependency would provide little protection against redescriptive success being mistaken for explanatory progress. By contrast, differentiated outcomes—residual gain, near-null gain, and null gain under an unchanged procedure—would provide preliminary evidence that dependency analysis can serve as a comparative diagnostic instrument. Such evidence would establish neither universal applicability nor explanatory superiority. Its contribution would be more limited: to show that explicit dependency analysis can be organised so that its value is assessed against, rather than presumed in advance of, the explanatory achievements of existing biological accounts.

Establishing that relationship requires greater precision about what established forms of explanation already capture. The next section therefore examines mechanistic, interventionist, counterfactual, difference-making, and pluralist approaches in order to locate dependency analysis within, rather than above or outside, the existing explanatory landscape.

## 2. Dependency Without Replacement: Relationship to Existing Explanation

The methodological role proposed for dependency analysis depends on maintaining a clear distinction between identifying dependencies and replacing the explanatory approaches through which those dependencies are already understood. Biological explanation does not confront dependency as an unrecognised relation awaiting a new vocabulary. Mechanistic, interventionist, counterfactual, difference-making, and pluralist approaches already capture different aspects of how outcomes vary with biological conditions. The relevant question is therefore comparative: what, if anything, becomes explanatorily available when dependency relations are made explicit that was not already adequately represented by the strongest established explanation?

Mechanistic explanation provides the most immediate constraint on this question. Mechanisms explain phenomena through organised entities and activities whose interactions produce, maintain, or otherwise constitute the phenomenon under investigation (Machamer, Darden, and Craver 2000). In biology, this explanatory strategy connects experimentally identifiable components with their activities, spatial and temporal organisation, and contribution to system behaviour (Darden 2006; Craver 2007). Mechanistic explanation therefore already represents many dependencies. An activity may depend on the presence or state of a component; the effect of one process may depend on its organisation with others; and a system-level outcome may depend on coordinated activities unfolding under particular conditions. Organisation is not extraneous to such explanation but can be integral to understanding how biological mechanisms exercise control and maintain coordinated activity (Bich and Bechtel 2021, 2022).

Dependency analysis consequently cannot claim explanatory gain simply by reformulating a mechanism as a set of dependency relations. The mechanism provides something indispensable: an account of the material entities, activities, interactions, and organisation through which relevant differences are realised. Explicit dependency analysis can complement this account only if it clarifies relations relevant to the explanandum that remain insufficiently integrated in the mechanistic explanation. Conversely, where a mechanism already specifies those relations adequately, dependency analysis should return no additional explanatory gain. This is a substantive constraint on the proposed method rather than a concession to be applied after the analysis.

Interventionist explanation places dependency even more explicitly at the centre of causal explanation. Woodward's interventionist framework characterises explanatory causal relations through patterns of invariance under interventions: roughly, whether appropriate changes to one variable would produce changes in another under specified conditions (Woodward 2003). This provides a disciplined form of what-if-things-had-been-different reasoning and connects explanation to experimentally tractable difference-making. For biological inquiry, where perturbation, knockout, inhibition, stimulation, environmental manipulation, and comparative experimental conditions are commonplace, intervention-sensitive evidence can provide especially strong grounds for claims that one process or condition makes a difference to another.

Any dependency methodology must therefore avoid presenting intervention sensitivity as its own innovation. If a proposed dependency claim amounts only to the assertion that changing X changes Y under specified conditions, an interventionist framework may already represent its explanatory content. The question for the present method is instead whether such intervention-sensitive relations, together with other relevant dependencies, are adequately integrated into the explanation of the specified biological outcome. Intervention evidence can strongly support a dependency claim without establishing that recasting the explanation around that dependency adds anything further.

Counterfactual approaches broaden this constraint. Explanation can be associated with information about how outcomes would differ under relevant alternative circumstances, and counterfactual accounts need not be confined to a narrow conception of causal intervention. Reutlinger (2016), for example, develops a counterfactual conception intended to encompass causal and non-causal scientific explanation. Whatever position is taken on the scope of such accounts, counterfactual structure already supplies a general language of dependency: if relevant conditions had been different, the explanandum or its occurrence would have differed in specified ways. The novelty of the present audit therefore cannot reside in introducing what-if-things-had-been-different reasoning into biological explanation. Counterfactual support is instead one means of assessing whether a proposed dependency is explanatorily grounded.

Biological difference-making introduces a further qualification. Waters (2007) argues for attention to causes that make actual differences in biological contexts, highlighting the importance of explanatory relevance relative to particular investigative problems. Biological systems contain innumerable causal relations, but not every causally implicated factor carries equal explanatory significance for every question. What matters depends in part on the contrast under investigation and on which differences illuminate the phenomenon of interest. For dependency analysis, this means that merely cataloguing relations of dependence would be explanatorily indiscriminate. The relevant relata, conditions, contrasts, and outcomes must be specified in relation to an explanandum. A dependency that is real but irrelevant to the explanatory contrast does not acquire significance merely by being made explicit.

Nor should this emphasis on difference-making be taken to reduce the proposed method to interventionism. Saatsi and Pexton (2013) question whether Woodward's account can straightforwardly supply a general theory covering both causal and non-causal explanation, illustrating the broader difficulty of identifying explanatory dependence with any single formal relation. More directly within biology, Fagan (2016) examines a mechanistic case in which explanatory omissions arise when an interventionist reconstruction fails to capture features relevant to the biological explanation. The appropriate conclusion here is deliberately limited. These arguments do not establish that interventionism fails as an explanatory framework, nor do they establish dependency analysis as its successor. They show why an audit concerned with explanatory integration should not prejudge all relevant dependencies as instances of one explanatory relation.

The same restraint applies to relations between mechanistic and organisational explanation. Mechanisms can be analysed through their component activities without treating organisation as a separate causal agency, while biological control can depend on the way those activities are integrated into a functioning organisation (Bich and Bechtel 2021, 2022). Dependency analysis can ask how such relations bear on the explanandum, but it cannot substitute an abstract relational description for the material processes that realise them. A dependency claim that cannot be connected to biological implementation would be weaker, not stronger, for having been stated at a more general level.

This plural structure of explanation is compatible with the broader methodological lesson of integrative pluralism. Mitchell (2002) argues that scientific complexity can require multiple explanatory models and levels of representation whose contributions need not collapse into a single privileged account. For present purposes, the important implication is that integration should not be equated with reduction to a uniform vocabulary. Mechanistic, intervention-sensitive, counterfactual, regulatory, and other explanatory resources may illuminate different relations within the same biological phenomenon. A comparative dependency analysis may help determine how those relations fit together without claiming that they become explanatory only after translation into dependency language.

The resulting relationship is therefore complementary but conditional. Mechanistic and other biological accounts specify how relevant processes and relations are materially realised; interventionist and counterfactual approaches provide resources for identifying difference-making under actual or possible variation; and pluralist approaches caution against assuming that explanatory adequacy requires reduction to one form. The Dependency-Hypothesis Audit asks a narrower question across these resources: are the dependencies relevant to the specified explanandum already adequately represented and integrated by the established explanation? Where they are, no additional explanatory gain should be claimed. Where they are not, the audit may identify residual explanatory integration, but that contribution must be demonstrated rather than presumed.

This also fixes the interpretation of a null result. The audit does not evaluate whether an entire biological phenomenon has been completely explained. A null verdict therefore cannot establish that no explanatory questions remain, that the relevant mechanisms are fully understood, or that competing biological accounts have been resolved. It establishes only that, relative to the explanandum, contrast, evidence, and established explanation examined, explicit dependency analysis has not demonstrated additional explanatory gain of the kind being tested. Null sensitivity is thus essential to the method's comparative function: without it, successful dependency description could too easily be mistaken for explanatory improvement.

Because dependencies are already represented through multiple established explanatory approaches, the remaining methodological requirement is to specify how additional explanatory integration can be distinguished systematically from redescription. The next section therefore sets out the neutral Dependency-Hypothesis Audit and the criteria by which candidate dependency claims, established explanatory anticipation, residual contribution, and null outcomes are assessed.

3. The Dependency-Hypothesis Audit

The methodological problem identified in the preceding sections requires an instrument that can assess dependency claims without presuming their explanatory value. The Dependency-Hypothesis Audit v0.1 is designed for this purpose. It is a neutral comparative procedure for asking whether explicit representation of dependency contributes explanatory integration beyond that already supplied by the strongest established explanation of a specified biological phenomenon. It does not provide a general theory of scientific explanation, and it does not determine whether a biological phenomenon is completely explained. Its target is narrower: the explanatory contribution, if any, produced by making a specified dependency structure explicit.

The audit proceeds through four functional domains: explanatory specification, evidential grounding, comparative explanatory assessment, and verdict control (Table 1). These domains are sequentially related but are not numerical stages. No score is assigned to individual criteria, and satisfying a larger number of criteria does not mechanically generate a stronger verdict. Instead, the audit requires an evidentially grounded comparison between a candidate dependency claim and what established explanation already provides.

3.1 Explanatory specification

The first requirement is to specify what is being explained. A dependency claim cannot be evaluated in abstraction from an explanandum: the biological outcome, capacity, transition, state, or pattern for which explanation is sought. The audit also requires an explicit foil or contrast wherever the explanatory question depends on why one outcome occurs rather than another. This constraint reflects the broader importance of explanatory contrasts and difference-making. Biological systems contain many causally relevant conditions, but explanatory relevance depends on which differences matter to the question being asked (Waters 2007).

The candidate dependency must then be stated by identifying its relata. Rather than saying generally that a biological phenomenon “depends on organisation,” for example, the analysis must specify what biological state or process depends on what other state, process, condition, or configuration, and under which relevant circumstances. The relation must also be characterised sufficiently to determine what kind of dependence is being proposed. It may involve causal production, enabling conditions, regulatory control, temporal ordering, conditional responsiveness, constitutive organisation, or another empirically defensible relation. This classification does not require all dependencies to be reduced to a single formal type. Its function is to prevent explanatory work from being assigned to an unspecified notion of dependence.

3.2 Evidential grounding

A well-specified dependency claim must next be connected to the biological processes through which the relevant relation is materially realised. Mechanistic explanation is especially important here because biological mechanisms identify organised entities and activities through which phenomena are produced or maintained (Machamer, Darden, and Craver 2000; Craver 2007). The audit does not treat dependency as an autonomous causal layer above those processes. A candidate dependency must either identify its material implementation or remain demonstrably compatible with the established biological account of that implementation.

Evidence from interventions or perturbations can provide particularly strong support. If altering one relevant condition systematically changes an outcome, the result can establish difference-making under specified conditions in a manner familiar from interventionist explanation (Woodward 2003). But intervention sensitivity has two different roles that must not be conflated. It can provide evidence that a dependency is real while providing no evidence that stating the relation explicitly adds explanatory value. If the established explanation already incorporates the intervention-sensitive relation, its translation into dependency language may add nothing.

Counterfactual support is treated similarly. Information about how an outcome would vary under relevant alternative conditions can clarify the dependency structure of an explanation, whether the relation is interpreted causally or within a broader counterfactual account (Reutlinger 2016). Yet counterfactual dependence is neither a novelty criterion nor an automatic marker of explanatory gain. It is evidence relevant to a dependency claim whose contribution must still be assessed comparatively.

The audit also records conditionality and temporal organisation. Biological difference-making frequently depends on context: developmental state, prior history, environmental conditions, physiological regime, timing, or the configuration of interacting processes. A relation that holds only under specified conditions should not be generalised beyond them. Similarly, the temporal ordering of dependencies may be explanatory where prior states constrain later possibilities or where perturbations have different consequences at different stages. These features are recorded insofar as they are supported by the case evidence; the audit does not infer them from dependency terminology itself.

3.3 Comparative explanatory assessment

Evidential grounding establishes that a dependency claim is defensible; it does not establish that the claim improves explanation. The third domain therefore provides the decisive comparative control. The audit identifies the strongest established explanation relevant to the specified explanandum and asks how much of the proposed dependency structure that explanation already represents.

This comparison is intentionally demanding. Mechanistic explanations may already specify dependencies through organised causal production. Interventionist accounts may explicitly represent how outcomes vary under manipulation. Counterfactual accounts may represent relevant alternative conditions, while regulatory or organisational explanations may already integrate relations among processes that would otherwise appear separately (Woodward 2003; Reutlinger 2016; Bich and Bechtel 2021). Heterogeneous explanatory resources may also legitimately coexist rather than awaiting reduction to one vocabulary (Mitchell 2002). The audit therefore asks not whether the word dependency appears in the established account, but whether the explanatory relation captured by the candidate dependency is already adequately represented.

This is the audit's central redescription control:

Successful representation of a biological phenomenon in dependency language does not itself constitute explanatory gain.

Explanatory gain requires a demonstrable difference between what the strongest established explanation already integrates and what becomes explanatorily available when the relevant dependency structure is made explicit. Such a difference might consist in bringing separately established relations into a comparison relevant to the explanandum, clarifying conditional or temporal dependencies that remain dispersed across explanatory resources, or identifying a dependency whose explanatory role is supported but not adequately integrated in the comparator account. These possibilities are not assumed to occur in any particular case.

Conversely, if mechanistic, regulatory, intervention-sensitive, or other established explanations already represent and integrate the relevant relations, explicit dependency analysis principally redescribes them. This remains so even when the dependency formulation is accurate, systematic, or convenient. Standardisation and comparative explicitness may constitute methodological utility without constituting substantive explanatory gain.

The audit consequently evaluates the integration of dependencies relevant to a specified explanandum and contrast, not the adequacy of an entire theory, mechanism, research programme, or biological field. This restriction is essential to interpreting its verdicts.

3.4 Verdict control and failure conditions

Audit v0.1 permits three qualitative forms of outcome. Residual explanatory gain is recorded where explicit dependency analysis contributes identifiable explanatory integration not already adequately represented by the established comparator. Null to minimal residual gain, or a near-null result, applies where explicit dependency analysis may improve comparative representation or standardisation but contributes little substantive explanatory integration. Null explanatory gain applies where the relevant dependencies are already adequately represented and dependency analysis principally redescribes them.

These categories are qualitative comparative verdicts, not points on a quantitative or ordinal scale. They do not imply that residual gain is a measurable quantity or that cases can be ranked by explanatory value. Their purpose is to prevent a binary assumption that dependency analysis either “works” or “fails” whenever it can describe a phenomenon.

The method also specifies conditions under which substantive explanatory gain has not been established. A dependency analysis fails this test when it merely renames established causal or regulatory relations; identifies dependencies already adequately integrated by existing explanation; cannot specify the relevant relata; lacks an appropriate explanatory contrast; cannot connect the proposed relation to material implementation; lacks adequate difference-making support where such evidence is required; produces no additional explanatory integration; or cannot distinguish its proposed contribution from established explanatory approaches. Critical assessments of attempts to assimilate diverse explanatory relations to interventionist reconstruction reinforce the importance of preserving such distinctions rather than treating one dependency relation as exhaustive (Fagan 2016).

These controls make null sensitivity a methodological requirement. A null verdict does not mean that the phenomenon is fully explained, that no scientific uncertainties remain, or that the established explanation cannot be improved in other respects. It means only that explicit dependency analysis has not demonstrated additional explanatory integration of the kind tested by the audit.

Finally, Audit v0.1 is framework-neutral with respect to APS. None of its specification criteria, evidential requirements, comparator procedures, failure conditions, or verdict categories requires APS premises. The audit must be capable of returning residual, near-null, or null outcomes independently, and no subsequent APS interpretation can convert one neutral verdict into another. This separation is necessary if the method is to provide evidence rather than merely reproduce conclusions supplied by the framework in which it was developed.

An instrument designed to distinguish explanatory gain from redescription cannot be assessed by demonstrating only cases in which it returns a positive result. It requires calibration against deliberately contrasting explanatory situations, including cases in which little or no residual contribution should be expected. Section 4 therefore specifies the comparative design and calibration functions of planarian regeneration, Pristionchus pacificus mouth-form polyphenism, and mammalian glucose homeostasis before the individual case analyses are presented.

## 4. Calibration Design

The Dependency-Hypothesis Audit is evaluated through three deliberately contrasting biological cases. The purpose of this design is calibration rather than representative sampling. The cases were selected because they place different demands on the same comparative procedure and therefore allow its capacity to distinguish explanatory integration from redescription to be examined. They do not constitute a representative sample of biological phenomena, and conclusions drawn from them cannot by themselves establish universal applicability of the method.

C01, planarian regeneration, provides the positive/residual calibration. Planarian regeneration is a well-developed experimental system in which extensive research has identified stem-cell activity, wound responses, positional information, regulatory signalling, and mechanisms involved in restoring anatomical pattern following injury (Reddien 2018). This makes the case demanding in a useful sense: dependency analysis cannot derive explanatory value merely from identifying relations that established regenerative biology already describes. C01 instead tests whether making the dependencies among established explanatory resources explicit can contribute identifiable integration relative to a specified regenerative explanandum. Its calibration function is therefore to test whether the audit is capable of detecting residual explanatory contribution even where substantial mechanistic knowledge already exists.

C02, Pristionchus pacificus mouth-form polyphenism, provides the transfer calibration. Alternative mouth morphologies in P. pacificus are regulated through a comparatively explicit developmental switch architecture linking environmental conditions, genetic regulation, dosage-sensitive relations, and alternative phenotypic outcomes. Experimental work on EUD-1 established a switch with strong effects on mouth-form determination, while subsequent work on SEUD-1 further characterised opposing dosage relationships within the regulatory architecture (Ragsdale et al. 2013; Bui, Ivers, and Ragsdale 2018). The relevance of C02 lies not simply in its difference from regeneration, but in the different explanatory configuration it presents. The case tests whether the unchanged audit can transfer from regenerative pattern restoration to environmentally responsive developmental polyphenism without presupposing that a successful application must yield substantial residual explanatory gain.

C03, mammalian glucose homeostasis, provides the null calibration. Glucose regulation is a mature physiological domain in which endocrine signalling, pancreatic function, hepatic and peripheral responses, feedback, compensation, and temporal changes between physiological states are extensively represented within established explanations (Röder et al. 2016). This makes the case deliberately stringent. The purpose is not to demonstrate that glucose homeostasis is completely explained; substantial empirical questions remain in any mature biological field. Rather, C03 asks whether the audit can recognise a situation in which the dependencies relevant to its specified explanandum are already sufficiently explicit and integrated that reformulating them as a dependency structure should yield no substantive additional explanatory contribution.

The inclusion of a null calibration is methodologically essential. A comparative method that identifies explanatory gain only in cases selected because they are favourable to the method cannot establish discrimination between explanatory integration and redescription. If dependency terminology can be applied to virtually any biological system, successful application alone provides little evidence of explanatory value. C03 therefore tests whether the audit can withhold a positive verdict when established explanation already performs the relevant integrative work. A null outcome is, in this respect, evidence about the discriminatory behaviour of the instrument rather than evidence of methodological failure.

C02 serves a different purpose from either endpoint. A comparison consisting only of a case expected to permit residual gain and a case designed to test a null result could show contrast without establishing that the procedure transfers effectively across explanatory contexts. The developmental-polyphenism case introduces a distinct biological domain in which many dependencies are already unusually explicit. Its role is to test whether the same criteria can be applied without alteration and without forcing the outcome toward either predetermined endpoint.

This requirement of an unchanged audit is a central design control. Audit v0.1 is applied to each case without retrospective modification of its criteria, evidential requirements, redescription control, or verdict logic. Differences among case outcomes must therefore arise from differences in the relationship between the available evidence, the specified explanandum, and the strongest established explanation—not from adjustments made to secure a preferred result. The case analyses may differ in biological content, but the comparative instrument does not.

Finally, the three calibration roles do not constitute an ordinal progression. C01, C02, and C03 are not ranked by biological complexity, organisational sophistication, quality of scientific explanation, or scientific importance. Nor does their sequence imply increasing or decreasing quantities of dependency. They are contrasting tests of a single methodological question: whether the same audit can discriminate among situations in which explicit dependency analysis may contribute differently relative to what established explanation already provides.

With the calibration functions fixed in advance and the audit held constant, the method can now be applied sequentially to the three cases. Section 5 begins with planarian regeneration as the positive/residual calibration.

## 5. Planarian Regeneration and Residual Explanatory Gain

Planarian regeneration provides the positive/residual calibration for the Dependency-Hypothesis Audit. The relevant explanandum is deliberately narrower than regeneration in general: the restoration of appropriate anterior–posterior anatomical pattern following injury. The corresponding contrast concerns conditions or perturbations under which appropriate polarity or pattern restoration is altered or fails. This specification matters because the audit does not ask whether dependency language improves the explanation of everything involved in planarian regeneration. It asks whether making particular dependencies explicit contributes explanatory integration relative to this defined regenerative outcome.

Contemporary planarian biology already supplies a substantial explanation of the processes relevant to this question. Regeneration depends on adult pluripotent stem cells, or neoblasts, which generate differentiated cell types and respond to regenerative demands following injury. Wounding initiates responses associated with tissue repair and regeneration, while positional information provides spatial specification required for appropriate replacement of missing structures. Anterior–posterior identity is regulated through signalling systems including Wnt-related processes and their antagonists, and the regenerative response coordinates cell production with positional instructions so that replacement occurs with appropriate anatomical identity (Reddien 2018). The explanatory starting point is therefore not an absence of mechanism, but a comparatively rich account of regenerative competence, injury response, positional specification, and pattern formation.

The first candidate dependency concerns the relation between regenerative competence and anatomical restoration. Neoblasts provide the cellular capacity required to generate missing tissues, but regenerative competence alone does not specify which structures should be restored or their orientation. The relevant outcome consequently depends not only on the availability and activity of regenerative cells but also on positional conditions under which their progeny contribute to appropriate pattern. This is not a newly identified relation. The distinction between cellular regenerative capacity and positional specification is already central to contemporary accounts of planarian regeneration (Reddien 2018). For the audit, its importance lies instead in fixing two distinct relata whose contributions must be integrated relative to the explanandum.

Positional information itself has identifiable material implementation. Muscle cells in planarians express position-control genes in spatially patterned domains and alter this expression following amputation, providing instructions relevant to regeneration (Witchley et al. 2013). This finding is especially informative for the present audit because it connects positional specification to a defined cellular substrate. The dependency under consideration is therefore not an abstract claim that anatomical form depends on “global organisation.” Spatial information relevant to regenerative outcomes is materially associated with cells and gene-expression patterns whose states change in response to injury.

Anterior–posterior restoration provides a more specific regulatory instance. Wnt-associated signalling contributes to posterior identity, while anterior-promoting processes including notum participate in establishing appropriate polarity after injury (Reddien 2018). Perturbation of these regulatory relations can alter regenerative outcomes, providing evidence that correct anatomical restoration depends on regulatory states rather than simply on tissue production. Again, dependency terminology does not discover this relation. The experimental literature already supplies the relevant causal and regulatory evidence. What the audit records is how this evidence bears on the specified contrast between appropriate polarity restoration and altered regenerative pattern.

Bioelectric evidence adds another experimentally grounded relation, but it requires particular caution. Durant et al. (2019) investigated early bioelectric signals associated with anterior–posterior polarity and showed that perturbing bioelectric state can alter downstream molecular responses and regenerative polarity. This supports a dependency between early physiological conditions and later anatomical outcomes under the experimental conditions examined. It does not establish bioelectric state as an autonomous organiser of regeneration, nor does it justify replacing molecular, cellular, or signalling explanations with a bioelectric one. Its relevance is narrower: early physiological state can constitute a materially implemented and perturbation-sensitive condition bearing on subsequent polarity specification.

These findings collectively provide strong evidential grounding for the candidate dependencies. Regenerative outcome is related to neoblast-mediated cellular competence, injury-responsive changes, spatial information expressed in muscle, anterior–posterior regulatory signalling, and, under experimentally supported conditions, early bioelectric state. The relations are not all of the same type. Some concern necessary regenerative capacities, some spatial specification, some regulatory difference-making, and some temporally early physiological conditions. The audit does not combine them into a newly proposed mechanism. Instead, it asks whether their relation to the common explanandum is already adequately integrated by the strongest established explanation.

That comparator must be represented strongly. Contemporary planarian research does considerably more than enumerate components. It explains how injury triggers regenerative responses, how neoblasts supply new cells, how positional information is encoded and revised, and how signalling systems contribute to the specification of appropriate anatomical identities (Reddien 2018; Witchley et al. 2013). Perturbation experiments provide evidence for the causal or regulatory relevance of particular processes, including early bioelectric conditions (Durant et al. 2019). Planarian regeneration has also been explicitly discussed as a problem of anatomical homeostasis in which biological processes restore large-scale anatomical outcomes following substantial perturbation (Levin, Pietak, and Bischof 2019). It would therefore be misleading to portray established explanation as a collection of isolated findings awaiting dependency analysis to make them biologically coherent.

This concession sharply restricts what can count as additional explanatory gain. The audit's redescription control asks: would explicit dependency analysis merely rename relations already adequately represented by this established account? For many individual relations, the answer is yes. Saying that polarity depends on Wnt-related regulation, that regeneration depends on neoblast competence, or that positional information depends on materially implemented expression patterns adds little if it merely restates what the relevant experiments and mechanistic accounts already establish. Similarly, describing perturbation-sensitive bioelectric effects as dependencies does not itself improve their explanation.

The possible residual contribution appears only when the analysis is kept relative to the specified explanandum. Restoration of appropriate anterior–posterior anatomy requires more than any one of the established relations considered separately. Regenerative competence must operate under conditions in which positional information is appropriately re-established; positional and regulatory processes must discriminate anterior from posterior outcomes; perturbation-sensitive physiological and signalling states can affect that specification; and these relations must converge on restoration of an anatomically appropriate result. Explicit dependency analysis places these established findings into a common structure organised by the contrast between successful restoration and altered pattern.

The resulting contribution is therefore integration, not discovery. Its explanatory value lies in clarifying that the specified regenerative outcome is conditional on the conjunction and temporal coordination of materially implemented capacities and regulatory relations that have often been investigated through different experimental questions. This can make explicit how evidence concerning stem-cell competence, positional specification, regulatory perturbation, and anatomical outcome jointly constrains the explanandum. The gain does not arise simply because the findings come from different studies; distribution across a literature is not itself an explanatory deficiency. It arises only insofar as their explicit integration makes clearer how the established dependencies jointly bear on the particular regenerative contrast.

This conclusion is consistent with, but not established by, the anatomical-homeostasis framing developed by Levin, Pietak, and Bischof (2019). That framing foregrounds the restoration of anatomical configuration and thereby helps identify a relevant explanatory target. The audit nevertheless applies an independent comparative test: whether explicit dependency structure contributes anything beyond what the strongest established biological explanation already integrates. No organisational vocabulary can substitute for that comparison.

The resulting case verdict is therefore deliberately bounded:

C01 VERDICT — PARTIAL / RESIDUAL EXPLANATORY GAIN

Partial is essential. The audit does not reveal previously unknown cellular components, signalling pathways, positional systems, or bioelectric phenomena. Much of the dependency structure is already anticipated, and often explicitly represented, by mechanistic and regulatory explanations. The residual contribution consists in making the explanandum-relative integration of these established relations more explicit: regenerative competence, positional specification, perturbation-sensitive regulation, and anatomical outcome can be represented together as conditions bearing on successful restoration rather than as independent discoveries.

This verdict applies only to the specified explanandum and comparator. It does not establish that dependency analysis improves every explanation of planarian regeneration, nor does it generalise automatically to regeneration in other organisms. Its significance for the present study is methodological: under the unchanged audit, a biologically well-explained case can still permit a bounded residual contribution without requiring established explanation to be weakened or displaced.

C01 therefore establishes the positive side of the calibration while preserving the redescription control. The next test is more demanding in a different way: whether the same audit transfers to a developmental system in which the relevant dependency architecture is already represented with greater explicitness. Section 6 applies the unchanged procedure to Pristionchus pacificus mouth-form polyphenism.

## 6. Pristionchus pacificus: Developmental Polyphenism and the Transfer Test

The second calibration asks whether the unchanged Dependency-Hypothesis Audit transfers from regeneration to a substantially different biological context without requiring a positive explanatory result. The explanandum is the developmental determination of alternative Pristionchus pacificus mouth morphologies under relevant environmental and regulatory conditions. P. pacificus develops alternative stenostomatous and eurystomatous mouth forms associated with different feeding capacities. The relevant contrast is therefore between these alternative developmental outcomes and the conditions under which development is biased toward one rather than the other.

This is a demanding transfer case because dependency is already unusually explicit in the established explanation. Mouth-form polyphenism is environmentally responsive, and earlier work connected developmental plasticity in Pristionchus with environmental sensing and endocrine signalling (Bento et al. 2010). Subsequent genetic analysis identified EUD-1, a sulfatase whose activity has a strong influence on the developmental switch controlling alternative mouth forms (Ragsdale et al. 2013). The explanandum is thus already framed through relations connecting environmental conditions, regulatory processes, and alternative phenotypic outcomes.

The regulatory architecture was subsequently resolved in considerably greater detail. NHR-40, a nuclear hormone receptor, functions in the developmental switch downstream of EUD-1, providing evidence for an ordered regulatory relationship rather than merely an association between genotype and phenotype (Kieninger et al. 2016). Work on SEUD-1 further demonstrated an opposing dosage-sensitive relationship within the switch: changes in the relative activity of regulatory factors can bias mouth-form outcome in opposing directions (Bui, Ivers, and Ragsdale 2018). These findings make explicit both relevant relata and experimentally tractable difference-making relations among them.

The switch also has a more complex genomic and regulatory context. Serobyan et al. (2016) showed that chromatin-level regulation and antisense transcription are associated with regulation of eud-1 and developmental plasticity. Sieriebriennikov et al. (2018) further characterised the developmental switch as involving a multi-gene locus rather than a single isolated switch gene. The resulting picture is consequently not one in which a solitary molecular determinant translates an environmental signal directly into phenotype. Multiple materially implemented regulatory relations contribute to the developmental decision.

Environmental dependence is likewise experimentally articulated rather than merely inferred from the existence of phenotypic plasticity. Population and developmental context can affect the environmental signals relevant to mouth-form determination. Werner et al. (2018), for example, demonstrated effects of adult-derived pheromonal conditions on juvenile phenotypes. Such findings provide evidence that developmental outcome depends on environmental and social conditions whose effects are mediated through identifiable biological processes. The precise relation between environment and phenotype is therefore conditional: the effect of a relevant signal depends on developmental context and the regulatory architecture through which it is processed.

For Audit v0.1, these studies provide substantial evidential grounding. The candidate dependencies include relations between environmental conditions and developmental response; EUD-1-associated regulation and mouth-form determination; opposing dosage-sensitive relations involving SEUD-1; downstream involvement of NHR-40; regulation associated with the multi-gene switch locus; and contextual effects of pheromonal conditions. Genetic perturbation, dosage manipulation, regulatory ordering, environmental manipulation, and phenotypic comparison all constrain the proposed relations. None needs to be inferred solely from an abstract dependency model.

The strength of this evidence also makes the established explanation a demanding comparator. The P. pacificus literature does not merely identify genes associated with two phenotypes. It already represents environmental sensitivity, regulatory architecture, gene ordering, opposing regulatory effects, dosage dependence, and the mapping of switch states onto alternative developmental outcomes. Threshold-like developmental determination is therefore not something first revealed by describing the system in dependency terms. Much of what a dependency analysis might initially appear to contribute is already part of the explanatory structure of the developmental-regulatory account.

The redescription control is consequently decisive. Does explicit dependency analysis reveal explanatory integration not already adequately represented by this account, or does it principally reorganise relations that are already explicit? At the level of individual candidate dependencies, the second answer predominates. Restating the influence of EUD-1 as a dependency of mouth-form outcome on EUD-1-associated regulation adds no substantive explanation. The same applies to the opposing dosage relation involving SEUD-1, the downstream involvement of NHR-40, or the environmental sensitivity of the developmental decision. These are experimentally established features of the existing explanation, not residual discoveries generated by the audit.

Nor does placing all these relations into a single dependency representation automatically create substantive gain. Doing so makes it possible to represent environmental, genetic, regulatory, dosage-sensitive, and phenotypic relations in a common comparative format. That is useful for the purposes of Audit v0.1 because it allows the same questions asked of planarian regeneration to be asked of a developmentally and mechanistically different system. But comparative standardisation is not equivalent to additional biological explanation. The distinction is particularly visible here because the strongest comparator already integrates much of the relevant dependency architecture.

A limited residual contribution can therefore be recognised only at the methodological margin. The audit makes explicit, within a common explanandum-relative representation, how environmental conditionality, switch architecture, regulatory ordering, dosage sensitivity, and phenotypic alternatives relate to the same developmental contrast. This may improve comparative explicitness across otherwise heterogeneous forms of evidence. Yet the biological relations doing the explanatory work are substantially those already established by the P. pacificus research programme. The audit does not materially change their interpretation or reveal an additional dependency required to explain the mouth-form decision.

This is precisely why C02 serves its intended transfer function. Audit v0.1 can be applied without modification to a system whose biology differs markedly from regenerative pattern restoration. Its requirements for specified relata, material implementation, difference-making evidence, comparison with the strongest established explanation, and redescription control remain applicable. At the same time, the procedure does not convert successful application into a positive explanatory verdict. Transfer is demonstrated by the applicability of the method, not by reproducing the C01 outcome.

The resulting verdict is therefore:

C02 VERDICT — NULL TO MINIMAL RESIDUAL EXPLANATORY GAIN / NEAR-NULL

The designation near-null is qualitative. It is not a numerical midpoint or an ordinal position between the C01 and C03 calibrations. It records the more limited conclusion that the audit provides comparative explicitness and standardisation while contributing little substantive explanatory integration beyond the established developmental-regulatory account.

Nor does this verdict imply that P. pacificus mouth-form development is completely explained. Questions about the mechanisms, environmental inputs, developmental dynamics, and evolutionary significance of the polyphenism can remain open independently of the audit. The verdict concerns only the incremental explanatory contribution of explicit dependency analysis relative to the specified explanandum and comparator.

C02 thus demonstrates transfer without requiring substantive explanatory gain. The strongest remaining test of discrimination is whether the unchanged audit can return a fully null result where the dependencies relevant to the explanandum are already extensively integrated by mature physiological explanation. Section 7 applies that test to mammalian glucose homeostasis.

## 7. Mammalian Glucose Homeostasis and the Null Test

Mammalian glucose homeostasis provides the null calibration for the Dependency-Hypothesis Audit. The specified explanandum is the maintenance and restoration of blood-glucose regulation across physiologically relevant perturbations and changing metabolic conditions. The relevant contrast is between effective regulatory compensation or restoration and conditions in which regulatory responses become impaired, insufficient, or dysregulated. This formulation does not treat glucose concentration as the output of a single feedback loop. It instead concerns a distributed physiological process involving pancreatic sensing and secretion, hormone action, organ and tissue responses, counter-regulation, and temporally changing metabolic conditions.

Dependency relations are already explicit throughout this explanatory domain. Pancreatic β cells respond to metabolic state through cellular processes coupling glucose metabolism and electrical activity to insulin secretion (Rorsman and Ashcroft 2018). Secreted insulin in turn affects glucose handling across multiple tissues, while variation in insulin sensitivity changes the relationship between circulating insulin and glucose regulation. Quantitative approaches to glucose–insulin dynamics have long made this reciprocal relationship experimentally tractable, including through measures designed to distinguish insulin sensitivity from pancreatic responsiveness (Bergman et al. 1979). The relevant physiology therefore already represents both stimulus-sensitive endocrine activity and tissue-dependent responses to endocrine signals.

These relations extend beyond insulin secretion and action. Hepatic glucose production and peripheral glucose uptake contribute differently to circulating glucose regulation, while insulin resistance involves altered responses across liver, muscle, adipose tissue, and other metabolic processes rather than a single uniform failure of insulin action (Petersen and Shulman 2018). The consequences of a given insulin state consequently depend on tissue condition and metabolic context. Established physiological explanation represents these conditional relations directly: the same endocrine signal need not produce the same regulatory consequence under different states of sensitivity or resistance.

Enteroendocrine modulation further illustrates the distributed character of the system. GLP-1 contributes to glucose regulation through effects including glucose-dependent stimulation of insulin secretion and suppression of glucagon secretion, linking nutrient intake and gastrointestinal signalling to pancreatic endocrine responses (Holst 2007). These effects introduce additional state dependence into the regulatory architecture. The influence of a signal depends on prevailing glucose conditions and its interaction with other components of glucose regulation rather than functioning as an isolated causal input.

Counter-regulation is equally important. Falling glucose concentrations recruit responses that oppose further decline, including glucagon and autonomic mechanisms. Recurrent hypoglycaemia can itself alter subsequent counter-regulatory responses, demonstrating that regulatory behaviour depends on physiological history as well as current glucose state (Cryer 2013). The system is therefore temporally organised: prior perturbations can change the response to later perturbations. This provides a particularly clear example of a dependency that might appear significant when stated abstractly—current regulatory response depends on previous physiological conditions—but which is already explicitly represented within established physiological explanation.

Feedback and compensation likewise extend beyond a simple relation between glucose and insulin. Pancreatic endocrine organisation includes interacting cellular and signalling processes contributing to glucose-responsive regulation (Röder et al. 2016). Work examining feedback within pancreatic regulation further supports the view that endocrine output emerges from dynamically interacting processes rather than an isolated sensor-effector pair (Garzilli and Itzkovitz 2018). Taken together, these accounts represent glucose homeostasis as a materially implemented, distributed and conditional regulatory architecture.

The candidate dependencies identified by Audit v0.1 are consequently well grounded. Blood-glucose state bears on pancreatic endocrine activity; insulin secretion bears on hepatic and peripheral glucose handling; the consequences of insulin depend on tissue sensitivity; incretin signalling modulates endocrine response under relevant conditions; falling glucose recruits counter-regulatory mechanisms; prior physiological states can modify subsequent responses; and compensatory processes affect whether perturbation is corrected or persists. These relations are supported through physiological measurement, perturbation, clinical and experimental contrasts, cellular analysis, and quantitative characterisation.

The critical issue, however, is not whether these are genuine dependencies. It is whether representing them explicitly as dependencies provides explanatory integration beyond the strongest established account.

That comparator is already unusually integrative. Contemporary glucose physiology relates cellular glucose sensing to endocrine secretion, endocrine signals to tissue responses, hepatic production to peripheral utilisation, gastrointestinal signals to pancreatic function, counter-regulatory processes to falling glucose, and changes in physiological state to altered regulatory behaviour. Insulin resistance and hypoglycaemic dysregulation additionally provide perturbation-sensitive contrasts through which failure or modification of these relations becomes experimentally and clinically visible (Cryer 2013; Petersen and Shulman 2018). The explanatory architecture is therefore neither merely componential nor reducible to a simple glucose–insulin negative-feedback loop. It already incorporates conditional, reciprocal, compensatory, and temporally organised relations.

Component anticipation must therefore be conceded completely. Dependency analysis does not identify pancreatic sensing, insulin action, tissue sensitivity, hepatic output, peripheral uptake, incretin modulation, counter-regulation, or compensatory feedback as previously missing explanatory factors. More importantly for the audit, relational anticipation is also extensive. Established physiology does not merely list these components; it explains their regulatory significance through the ways changes in one condition alter, recruit, constrain, or compensate through others.

The redescription test can therefore be applied directly: does explicit dependency analysis make explanatorily available any relevant integration that is not already adequately represented by the strongest established physiological explanation?

For the specified explanandum and comparator, the answer is no. Recasting glucose-responsive insulin secretion as a dependency, for example, accurately represents a physiological relation but adds no integration beyond an account that already explains secretion as conditional on metabolic and cellular state. Representing glucose regulation as dependent on tissue insulin sensitivity similarly restates a relation central to established explanations of insulin action and resistance. The same applies to incretin modulation, counter-regulatory response, physiological history, and feedback. Even when these relations are collected within the common structure of Audit v0.1, their integration relative to maintenance and restoration of glucose regulation is already substantially present in the comparator.

This distinguishes C03 from a case in which common representation exposes an explanandum-relative relation that remains insufficiently integrated. Here the dependencies are not merely recoverable from the established explanation; they are among its organising explanatory relations. Describing them uniformly can aid comparison with other cases, but comparative standardisation does not constitute residual biological explanation.

The resulting verdict is therefore:

C03 VERDICT — NULL EXPLANATORY GAIN

The verdict does not mean that dependency analysis inaccurately represents glucose homeostasis. On the contrary, the audit can represent the relevant physiological relations successfully. It means that accuracy of dependency representation has not generated additional explanatory integration. The strongest established comparator already integrates the dependencies relevant to the specified explanandum, and no residual contribution of the tested kind has been demonstrated.

The scope of this conclusion is equally important. It does not imply that glucose homeostasis is completely understood, that current physiological models are exhaustive, that no important empirical problems remain, or that dependencies are biologically unimportant. Nor does it establish that dependency analysis could never contribute to a differently specified question about glucose regulation. The null is relative to this explanandum, contrast, evidence base, and comparator.

Precisely for that reason, C03 strengthens the methodological calibration. An audit that generated explanatory gain whenever a biological system could be represented in dependency terms would provide no reliable protection against redescription. Under unchanged Audit v0.1, however, successful dependency representation can be separated from substantive explanatory gain. The method is capable of withholding a positive verdict even in a system richly structured by dependencies.

With residual, near-null, and null outcomes now obtained under the same audit, the three calibration cases can be compared directly. Section 8 examines what this differentiation establishes—and does not establish—about diagnostic capacity, transferability, discrimination, and residual explanatory contribution.

## 8. Comparative Results: Gain, Near-Null, and Null

The three calibrations permit the first direct assessment of the Dependency-Hypothesis Audit as a comparative instrument. Their significance does not lie simply in producing different verdicts. More importantly, the same Audit v0.1 was applied without alteration to three biologically and explanatorily contrasting cases. The resulting pattern allows four methodological properties to be assessed: diagnostic capacity, procedural transferability, discrimination including null sensitivity, and the identification of residual explanatory contribution.

8.1 Diagnostic capacity

Across all three cases, biologically real dependencies could be specified and grounded in established evidence. Yet the existence of those dependencies did not determine the audit verdict. This distinction is central. Biologically real dependency, successful representation in dependency terms, comparative methodological usefulness, and substantive residual explanatory integration are not equivalent achievements.

C01 showed that explicit dependency analysis could contribute bounded integration relative to a specified regenerative explanandum. C02 showed that an equally successful dependency representation could provide comparative explicitness while adding little substantive explanatory integration. C03 showed that dependency representation could remain biologically accurate while adding none of the explanatory integration being tested.

The audit therefore did not use the presence of dependency as evidence for its own explanatory success. Its diagnostic function instead arose from comparing what dependency analysis made explicit with what the strongest established explanation already represented. This is the principal protection against mistaking redescriptive success for explanatory improvement.

8.2 Transferability

The three cases also provide preliminary evidence that the procedure can transfer across biological domains. Audit v0.1 was applied unchanged to regenerative pattern restoration, developmental polyphenism, and physiological homeostasis. In each case it was possible to specify an explanandum and contrast, identify candidate dependency relata, assess material and difference-making evidence, construct an established explanatory comparator, and apply the same redescription control.

C02 is particularly informative because transfer did not require reproduction of the C01 verdict. The developmental-regulatory architecture of P. pacificus already makes environmental sensitivity, switch relations, regulatory ordering, dosage effects, and alternative phenotypic outcomes comparatively explicit. The audit nevertheless remained applicable while withholding a substantial residual-gain claim. Procedural transferability must therefore be distinguished from explanatory gain: a method can transfer successfully even where its application shows that little additional explanation is contributed.

This constitutes preliminary cross-domain transfer, not universal applicability. Three purposively selected calibrations cannot establish that the procedure will remain useful across all biological explananda or explanatory contexts.

8.3 Discrimination and null sensitivity

The differentiated verdicts provide the clearest evidence of discrimination. C01 permits partial/residual explanatory gain; C02 returns null to minimal residual gain/near-null; and C03 returns null explanatory gain. These categories are qualitative outcomes of case-specific comparisons. They do not constitute an ordinal scale, numerical gradient, or progression in biological complexity or explanatory adequacy.

Their methodological importance instead lies in the audit's capacity to withhold positive conclusions. In C02, successful application did not require substantial explanatory gain. In C03, the dependencies relevant to the explanandum were already so extensively represented and integrated by established physiological explanation that explicit dependency analysis principally redescribed them. The resulting null was therefore not a failure of the audit. It was an outcome the audit had to be capable of producing if its redescription control was to have discriminative force.

Null sensitivity is consequently part of the evidence for diagnostic discrimination. An instrument that always identified residual gain whenever it successfully identified dependencies would provide little basis for distinguishing explanatory contribution from the applicability of its own vocabulary.

8.4 Residual contribution

The strongest conclusion supported by the comparison concerns the status of explanatory gain itself:

The explanatory contribution of explicit dependency analysis is residual and comparator-relative: it arises only where the relevant dependency structure contributes integration not already adequately supplied by the strongest established explanation.

Across these three cases, the scope for residual contribution diminished where established explanation already represented and integrated the dependencies relevant to the specified explanandum more explicitly. C01 left some integrative work available to the audit; C02 left very little; C03 left none of the kind being tested. This pattern should not be elevated into a universal law relating dependency analysis to scientific maturity. It is a result of this three-case calibration and requires testing against further cases.

The calibration therefore supports a bounded set of preliminary claims. Audit v0.1 displays diagnostic capacity across the tested cases; it transfers procedurally across the three domains without alteration; it discriminates between residual, near-null, and null outcomes; it demonstrates null sensitivity; and it can identify residual explanatory contribution relative to an explicit comparator rather than presuming such contribution from the presence of dependency.

Equally important are the conclusions the calibration does not warrant. Three purposively selected cases do not establish universal validity, representative biological coverage, or statistical validation. They do not show that dependency analysis is explanatorily superior to mechanistic, interventionist, regulatory, or other established approaches, or that it should replace them. The results establish no new ontology of dependency, provide no empirical confirmation of APS, and do not imply that all biological explanations benefit from explicit dependency analysis.

The calibration thus establishes something narrower but methodologically consequential: under the conditions tested, a common dependency audit can distinguish successful representation from residual explanatory contribution and can return a null when established explanation already performs the relevant integrative work. What remains is to determine what contribution such a discriminating instrument can make to the practice and evaluation of biological explanation. Section 9 addresses that question directly.

## 9. What the Audit Contributes

The three-case calibration establishes a deliberately limited result: Audit v0.1 can distinguish, across the three tested cases, between successful dependency representation and residual explanatory gain. Its contribution should be understood from that result rather than from any claim that dependency itself provides a new basis for biological explanation. Dependencies are already pervasive in biological science, and established explanatory approaches already identify, test, and integrate many of them. The question addressed here is instead whether a controlled comparison can determine when making those dependencies explicit contributes something further.

9.1 Minimum novelty: the comparative procedure

The article claims no novelty for dependency as a feature of biological systems, counterfactual dependence, intervention-sensitive difference-making, mechanistic organisation, regulatory relations, explanatory contrasts, or explanatory pluralism. Each already has an established place within scientific explanation. Mechanistic accounts specify organised entities, activities, and relations; interventionist and counterfactual approaches analyse difference-making; and pluralist approaches recognise the legitimate coexistence of heterogeneous explanatory resources (Machamer, Darden, and Craver 2000; Woodward 2003; Mitchell 2002).

The minimum defensible novelty lies instead in the explicit comparative procedure developed here: candidate dependency claims are assessed against the strongest established explanation of a specified explanandum, with residual, near-null, or null verdicts determined by whether explicit dependency analysis contributes additional explanandum-relative integration.

The redescription control is central to that procedure. Without it, almost any well-established biological relationship could be reformulated as a dependency and then presented as evidence for the explanatory value of dependency analysis. Audit v0.1 reverses that presumption. Successful reformulation establishes only that the dependency representation is applicable. Additional explanatory contribution must be demonstrated after what established explanation already provides has been conceded.

9.2 Comparative-methodological gain

The principal contribution is consequently methodological rather than a substantive biological discovery. Audit v0.1 provides a common procedure through which different explanatory contexts can be asked the same controlled questions: What exactly is the explanandum and relevant contrast? What dependency is proposed, and between which relata? How is that relation materially implemented and evidentially supported? What does the strongest established explanation already represent? After component and relational anticipation are conceded, what explanatory contribution remains?

Applying these questions consistently makes comparison possible without requiring the biological systems themselves to share mechanisms, regulatory architectures, or characteristic forms of evidence. The three calibration cases differ substantially in those respects. Their comparability is methodological: each candidate dependency analysis is subjected to the same burden of demonstrating what it contributes relative to an established comparator.

This also explains why a near-null or null biological verdict can coexist with methodological usefulness. A procedure that makes it possible to identify redescription reliably can perform useful comparative work even when it adds nothing substantive to the explanation of the biological phenomenon under examination. Methodological gain therefore cannot be inferred from the positivity of the biological verdict.

9.3 Three different kinds of outcome

The calibration supports a distinction among substantive explanatory gain, comparative-methodological gain, and mere redescription.

Substantive explanatory gain occurs only when making dependency structure explicit contributes explanandum-relative integration not already adequately supplied by the strongest comparator. C01 provides the bounded example in the present calibration: the gain is residual because extensive component and relational anticipation must first be conceded.

Comparative-methodological gain is different. It occurs where applying a common procedure improves disciplined comparison, exposes whether apparent novelty survives the redescription control, or standardises assessment across explanatory contexts. Such gain can remain when substantive biological contribution is minimal or absent. C02 and C03 are therefore informative applications even though they do not reproduce C01's residual result.

Mere redescription is narrower still. It occurs when dependency terminology reorganises relations that are already adequately represented and integrated without contributing either substantive integration or a relevant comparative function. The distinction matters because synthesis alone is not sufficient for explanatory novelty. Bringing known findings together can be useful, but usefulness and explanatory advance require separate assessment.

9.4 Complementarity with established explanation

Audit v0.1 is therefore complementary to established explanatory approaches. Mechanistic, regulatory, intervention-sensitive, counterfactual, and other biological explanations supply much of the evidence that makes the audit possible. The procedure neither replaces these approaches nor adjudicates which general theory of scientific explanation is correct.

Its question is more restricted:

Given the strongest established explanation, what additional explanatory work—if any—is performed by making the relevant dependency structure explicit?

This formulation deliberately permits the answer to be none. Indeed, the ability to reach that answer is integral to the method. An audit that treated successful dependency representation as sufficient evidence of explanatory improvement would reproduce the very problem it was designed to diagnose.

9.5 A diagnostic rather than replacement methodology

Audit v0.1 is best characterised as a diagnostic comparative instrument. It is not a universal explanatory schema, an ontology of dependency, a replacement for mechanistic explanation, or a measure of the total adequacy of an explanation. Its verdict concerns only the incremental contribution of explicit dependency analysis relative to a specified explanandum and comparator.

This diagnostic role imposes a burden of proof on claims of explanatory novelty. A new vocabulary does not by itself establish a new explanation. Nor does assembling established findings into a new representation necessarily demonstrate explanatory gain. Candidate dependency analyses must show what becomes explanatorily available that was not already adequately integrated by the comparator. Where that difference cannot be demonstrated, substantive novelty should not be claimed.

This burden is intentionally conservative. It protects the procedure against self-confirmation while also protecting established biological explanation against artificial weakening. The comparator must be represented in its strongest reasonable form; otherwise residual gain can be manufactured simply by omitting explanatory resources already available in the literature.

9.6 Strongest warranted contribution

The strongest contribution supported by the present calibration can therefore be stated as follows:

This study provides preliminary evidence that an explicit, comparator-controlled dependency audit can function as a transferable diagnostic method for distinguishing residual explanatory integration from redescription across contrasting biological explanatory contexts.

Each qualification matters. The evidence is preliminary because the audit has been calibrated across three purposively selected cases. It is comparator-controlled because explanatory contribution cannot be assessed independently of what established explanation already provides. It is diagnostic because the method evaluates incremental contribution rather than supplying a replacement explanation. And transferable refers only to demonstrated procedural transfer across the three tested domains—regenerative pattern restoration, developmental polyphenism, and physiological homeostasis—not to universal applicability across biology.

The differentiated outcomes are themselves central to this contribution. The method permitted residual gain, near-null gain, and null gain without changing its criteria. That pattern provides preliminary evidence that the audit can distinguish the explanatory contribution of explicit dependency analysis rather than merely documenting the presence of dependencies.

9.7 Future testing

The contribution remains open to further testing. Confidence would increase if the unchanged procedure proved usable across additional biological domains and by investigators independently selecting comparators and applying the audit criteria. Particularly informative tests would include cases in which the strongest comparator is contested, cases containing several competing established explanations, and cases where the available explanation is comparatively weak.

Such applications could also expose limitations. If comparator selection systematically determines the verdict, if the audit cannot be applied consistently by independent investigators, or if it fails to distinguish residual contribution from redescription in less controlled cases, its methodological scope would require corresponding restriction. These possibilities are tests of the proposed contribution, not problems to be resolved by redefining the audit after the fact.

Article 1 therefore establishes a bounded methodological result rather than a general explanatory theory. That result must now be assessed against the limitations of the present calibration itself. Section 10 considers the consequences of purposive case selection, comparator dependence, evidential constraints, scope restrictions, and the failure conditions under which the Dependency-Hypothesis Audit should not be regarded as demonstrating explanatory gain.

## 10. Limitations, Scope, and Failure Conditions

The three-case calibration supports a bounded methodological contribution, but several limitations constrain its interpretation. These limitations concern not only the amount of evidence presently available but also features intrinsic to comparator-controlled assessment. They therefore define the conditions under which conclusions from Audit v0.1 should be accepted, qualified, or withheld.

10.1 Comparator dependence

The principal methodological limitation is comparator dependence. Audit verdicts are explicitly relative to what the strongest established explanation already represents and integrates. Residual explanatory gain therefore cannot be identified independently of the comparator against which it is assessed.

This creates several sources of sensitivity. Different reasonable constructions of the established explanation could alter what appears to remain for explicit dependency analysis. Omitting relevant explanatory resources could artificially manufacture residual gain by attributing to dependency analysis integration that the literature already provides. Conversely, an excessively inclusive comparator could combine heterogeneous explanatory resources so comprehensively that a genuinely useful contribution becomes difficult to distinguish. The problem may be particularly acute where several established explanatory approaches coexist without a single uncontested synthesis.

The present study controls this risk by constructing each comparator in its strongest reasonable form and conceding both component and relational anticipation before assigning residual gain. This does not eliminate comparator dependence. Comparator specification is itself an interpretive methodological task. Whether different investigators can construct sufficiently similar comparators and reach compatible audit verdicts remains an important question for subsequent testing.

10.2 Purposive case selection

C01–C03 were purposively selected calibration cases. Their function was to test whether an unchanged audit could differentiate among contrasting relationships between dependency representation and established explanation. They were not selected to constitute a representative sample of biological phenomena.

The design therefore supports no statistical inference about biology generally. It cannot establish how frequently dependency analysis provides residual explanatory gain, how frequently null outcomes should be expected, or whether the three verdict categories encountered here exhaust the possible outcomes of future applications. Purposive selection is appropriate for an initial calibration because contrasting cases expose whether the procedure can discriminate among intended outcomes. That design advantage simultaneously restricts generalisation beyond the cases tested.

10.3 Calibration size and domain coverage

Three cases provide only preliminary evidence of cross-domain procedural transfer. Regenerative pattern restoration, developmental polyphenism, and physiological homeostasis differ substantially, but they represent only a small subset of biological explanatory contexts.

The present result is consequently limited to demonstrating that Audit v0.1 remained applicable across these three domains without modification. It does not establish that the same specifications, comparator procedures, or verdict distinctions will remain adequate across biological explanation generally. Broader domain coverage would be required before stronger claims of transferability could be justified.

10.4 Evidential dependence

Audit v0.1 does not generate the biological evidence on which its verdicts depend. It evaluates dependency claims using evidence and explanations supplied by existing research. The reliability of an audit is therefore constrained by the quality, scope, and maturity of the underlying literature.

Where evidence is weak, candidate dependencies may be difficult to specify. Where mechanistic or regulatory knowledge is incomplete, the strongest available comparator may itself be unstable. Residual gain identified under those conditions may subsequently diminish if later research incorporates the relevant relation into established explanation. The converse is also possible: new evidence may reveal dependencies that an earlier comparator could not represent.

Audit verdicts are therefore potentially revisable as biological knowledge changes. A verdict records the relation between a specified dependency analysis and an evidentially supported comparator at a particular state of inquiry; it should not be treated as permanently fixing the explanatory status of the biological phenomenon.

10.5 Explanandum and contrast dependence

The audit is also deliberately explanandum-relative. A verdict concerns a specified explanatory target and, where appropriate, a specified contrast. Changing either can change which dependencies are relevant and what the comparator must integrate.

A null result for one question therefore does not establish that dependency analysis contributes nothing to explaining the biological system as a whole. A different contrast, temporal frame, experimental condition, or explanandum could produce a different assessment. Correspondingly, the residual contribution identified for C01 cannot be extended automatically from restoration of anterior–posterior pattern to planarian regeneration generally. Case verdicts must remain attached to the explanatory questions through which they were generated.

10.6 Qualitative verdicts and reproducibility

Audit v0.1 deliberately does not assign numerical scores to explanatory gain. Residual, near-null, and null verdicts depend instead on reasoned comparison between the candidate dependency analysis and the established comparator. This introduces a reproducibility challenge: investigators may disagree about whether a remaining contribution is substantively integrative, principally methodological, or redescriptive.

Numerical scoring would not automatically remove this difficulty. Quantification would still require prior judgements about which features should be measured, how they should be weighted, and what thresholds distinguish explanatory categories. Assigning numbers without independent justification could replace explicit qualitative judgement with spurious precision.

Nevertheless, qualitative assessment cannot be exempt from reproducibility requirements. Independent application and inter-investigator comparison will be necessary to determine whether the audit's criteria constrain judgement sufficiently for its verdicts to be methodologically reliable.

10.7 Failure conditions

The audit's failure conditions specify circumstances in which substantive explanatory gain should not be claimed. Dependency analysis fails to establish such gain where it:

merely renames established causal or regulatory relations;
identifies dependencies already adequately integrated by existing explanation;
cannot specify the relevant relata;
lacks an appropriate explanatory contrast;
lacks material implementation;
lacks adequate difference-making support where such support is required;
produces no additional explanatory integration; or
cannot distinguish its proposed contribution from established explanatory approaches.

These are not peripheral cautions. They constrain the interpretation of a completed audit. A biologically plausible dependency representation can satisfy some evidential requirements while nevertheless failing to justify a positive explanatory claim.

10.8 Limits of the present contribution

The narrowest defensible conclusion is therefore that, within this three-case calibration, Audit v0.1 demonstrated procedural applicability across contrasting biological domains and differentiated residual, near-null, and null explanatory outcomes relative to specified comparators.

The study does not establish general validity, universal applicability, independent reproducibility, superiority to existing explanatory methodologies, a complete taxonomy of biological dependency relations, or a quantitative measure of explanatory gain. Each would require evidence beyond the present calibration.

These limitations leave the Article 1 result intact but sharply bounded. With its evidential reach, comparator dependence, scope restrictions, qualitative judgement, and failure conditions now explicit, the final section can state the article's conclusion at exactly the level warranted by the calibration.

## 11. Conclusion

Dependency is already pervasive in biological explanation. Mechanistic, intervention-sensitive, counterfactual, regulatory, and organisational accounts identify relations in which biological outcomes depend on particular processes, conditions, interactions, and differences. The methodological problem is therefore not whether biological phenomena can be represented in dependency terms. It is when making dependency structure explicit contributes additional explanatory integration, and when it merely redescribes relations already adequately represented by established explanation.

This study addressed that problem by developing the neutral Dependency-Hypothesis Audit v0.1. The audit requires a candidate analysis to specify its explanandum and relevant contrast, identify the proposed dependency and its relata, establish material implementation and appropriate evidential support, and compare the resulting representation with the strongest established explanation. Most importantly, it imposes a redescription control: successful representation of a phenomenon in dependency terms does not itself constitute explanatory gain. The same procedure was applied without alteration to three purposively selected biological calibration cases.

The resulting verdicts were differentiated. C01, planarian regeneration, produced partial/residual explanatory gain. C02, Pristionchus pacificus mouth-form polyphenism, produced null-to-minimal residual explanatory gain, or a near-null result. C03, mammalian glucose homeostasis, produced null explanatory gain. These outcomes are qualitative and comparator-relative rather than points on an ordinal scale. Their significance lies in the fact that the audit did not treat successful dependency representation as sufficient evidence of explanatory gain. All three systems could be represented through dependency relations, yet the explanatory contribution of doing so differed.

Within this three-case calibration, the governing question can therefore be answered in bounded form: explicit dependency analysis contributes explanatory gain only where it makes available explanandum-relative integration not already adequately supplied by the strongest established explanation. Where those dependencies are already adequately represented and integrated, dependency analysis is redescriptive rather than substantively explanatory. This conclusion describes the comparative result obtained here; it is not proposed as a universal law of scientific explanation.

The principal contribution is correspondingly methodological. The study provides preliminary evidence that an explicit, comparator-controlled dependency audit can function as a transferable diagnostic method for distinguishing residual explanatory integration from redescription across contrasting biological explanatory contexts. Transferability has been demonstrated only across the three domains examined here: regenerative pattern restoration, developmental polyphenism, and physiological homeostasis. The audit remains diagnostic and complementary. It assesses what, if anything, explicit dependency analysis contributes relative to established explanation; it neither replaces those explanations nor proposes a new ontology of dependency.

The null calibration is particularly important to this conclusion. C03 demonstrates that a dependency analysis can be biologically accurate, empirically grounded, and successfully completed while nevertheless adding no substantive explanatory integration relative to its comparator. The ability to return null is a methodological requirement, not a failure of the method. Without that possibility, dependency analysis would risk becoming self-confirming: identifying dependencies would itself become sufficient to establish the explanatory value of identifying them.

The contribution of the audit should therefore remain deliberately constrained. Its value does not lie in establishing that biological systems contain dependencies, a fact already embedded throughout biological explanation. Nor does a common dependency vocabulary itself constitute explanatory advance. The contribution tested here is more specific: a disciplined comparative procedure for determining whether explicit dependency analysis leaves genuine explanatory work after the strongest established explanation has been fully acknowledged. On the evidence of this calibration, the value of making biological dependencies explicit lies precisely in determining when doing so actually adds explanatory work—and when it does not.
