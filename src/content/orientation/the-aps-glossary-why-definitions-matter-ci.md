## Introduction

The APS Glossary is the conceptual foundation of APS. It defines the core terms used throughout the framework and clarifies how those terms relate to one another within a unified explanatory framework.

In biology, definitions are often treated as secondary to empirical investigation. Terms such as life, function, adaptation, and information are widely used, but are not always defined in a consistent or explicit way. This can lead to ambiguity, where different explanations rely on different implicit assumptions about what these terms mean.

APS treats definitions as central to explanation. Biological theory depends on a clear account of what is being explained, which requires that key concepts are defined consistently and in alignment with the organisation of living systems.

In APS, life is defined as viability-oriented, constraint-closed organisation: the organisation through which a system sustains its own persistence. This definition establishes the basis for understanding agency, normativity, function, and evolution within a single framework.

The glossary therefore functions not as a list of terms, but as an interconnected system of concepts. Each entry contributes to a broader explanatory framework in which definitions are linked, mutually supporting, and grounded in a shared account of biological organisation.

Using the glossary ensures that APS content remains consistent, transparent, and conceptually coherent. It provides a clear reference point for understanding how the framework defines its central ideas and how those ideas are applied across different domains of biology.

## Why APS Readers Should Begin with the Glossary

APS uses many familiar words, including life, agency, function, purpose, cognition, value, morality, and significance. These words often carry multiple meanings in biology, philosophy, and everyday language.

Within APS, however, such terms are defined in precise and controlled ways. Their meanings are not determined by common usage but by their place within the explanatory architecture of the framework.

As a result, readers who rely solely on ordinary-language interpretations may misunderstand the claims being made. A term that appears familiar may perform a specialised explanatory role within APS.

The glossary therefore serves a function beyond simple reference. It establishes the meanings through which APS explanations are constructed and interpreted. Understanding those meanings provides the foundation for understanding the framework itself.

For this reason, new readers are encouraged to consult glossary entries whenever they encounter unfamiliar terminology or concepts whose meaning appears broader or narrower than expected.

**Key Point**

The APS glossary is not simply a reference tool. It is the starting point for understanding how the framework is constructed.

## Canonical Definitions and Conceptual Discipline

APS definitions are not interchangeable descriptions or approximate summaries. Each definition functions as a canonical anchor within a controlled explanatory system. The wording of these definitions is therefore not stylistic but constitutive: small changes in phrasing can alter the conceptual structure of the framework.

To preserve coherence across the APS corpus, key terms are defined using a constrained vocabulary in which core expressions-such as *viability-oriented, constraint-closed organisation* - are treated as stable reference points. These expressions specify the organisational conditions that underlie biological explanation and are used consistently across articles, glossary entries, and research papers.

This discipline ensures that concepts do not drift as they are applied in different contexts. Definitions can be extended or elaborated, but such extensions must preserve their canonical structure and dependencies. In this way, the glossary does not merely describe terms; it stabilises the explanatory grammar of the framework.

**Key Point**
APS definitions are not flexible descriptions - they are canonical constraints that preserve the coherence of the framework.

## Why Precision Matters in APS

APS does not seek to regulate everyday language. Rather, it distinguishes between descriptive flexibility and explanatory commitment. In ordinary contexts, biological terms may be used loosely or metaphorically. In scientific explanation, however, the meaning of key terms must remain stable if claims are to be comparable, testable, and cumulative.

For this reason, APS treats core definitions as canonical anchors within a controlled explanatory grammar. This is not unusual in science: disciplines such as physics and chemistry depend on stable definitions to ensure that results can be interpreted consistently across contexts. APS extends this principle to biological explanation, where conceptual ambiguity has historically been tolerated.

The aim is not to restrict expression, but to preserve coherence. Definitions can be elaborated and applied in different contexts, but their core structure must remain stable. In this way, precision supports, rather than limits, the practical use of biological concepts.

**Key Point**
APS constrains meaning in explanation, not language in general.

## From Definitions to System

The APS glossary does not function as a list of independent entries. Its definitions form an organised conceptual system in which each term is constrained by its relations to others.

This means that:
- definitions cannot be modified in isolation
- conceptual changes propagate across the framework
- coherence depends on maintaining these relationships

## Definitions and Dependency Pathways

APS explanations increasingly make use of dependency pathways. These pathways express relationships of explanatory dependence between concepts and help clarify how different aspects of biological organisation are related within the framework.

For example:

Life  
↓  
Agency  
↓  
Biological Evaluation  
↓  
Significance

and:

Life  
↓  
Agency  
↓  
Process  
↓

Such pathways do not describe temporal sequences, causal chains, or stages through which systems pass. Rather, they identify concepts whose explanatory roles depend upon other concepts within the framework.

These relationships can only be articulated if the concepts involved possess stable meanings. If definitions shift from one context to another, dependency claims become ambiguous and pathways lose explanatory value.

The glossary therefore provides more than terminology. It supplies the conceptual stability required for pathway-based explanation. Definitions establish the meanings from which dependency relationships can be identified, evaluated, and refined.

Dependency pathways are therefore built upon conceptual foundations established in the glossary rather than functioning independently of them.

**Key Point**

Stable definitions make dependency pathways possible.

## Definitions and Explanatory Grammar

APS does not treat definitions as isolated statements about individual concepts. Definitions also help determine how concepts may be related within explanations.

For example, the APS definitions of Life, Agency, Biological Evaluation, Significance, Cognition, Values, and Social Norms make it possible to construct explanatory relationships between these concepts. Because their meanings are stabilised, explanations can be developed without repeatedly redefining the terms involved.

In this respect, definitions contribute to what APS calls explanatory grammar: the structured set of conceptual relationships through which explanations are constructed, evaluated, and communicated.

Just as grammatical rules help organise language into meaningful sentences, explanatory grammar helps organise concepts into meaningful explanations. It does not determine what must be explained, but it helps regulate how explanations are formulated and connected.

The glossary therefore performs a role beyond definition. It helps establish the conceptual conditions under which coherent explanation becomes possible.

**Key Point**

The APS glossary stabilises the explanatory grammar through which APS explanations are constructed.

## Conclusion

The APS glossary serves several interconnected functions within the framework.

It provides a reference point for the definitions used throughout APS. It helps maintain consistency across articles, glossary entries, and research projects. It supports dependency pathways by stabilising the concepts through which explanatory relationships are articulated. It also contributes to the explanatory grammar through which APS explanations are constructed and connected.

For these reasons, the glossary is more than a collection of definitions. It functions simultaneously as a reference resource, a conceptual map, and part of the explanatory infrastructure of the framework.

Understanding APS therefore begins not only with its theories and pathways, but with the concepts that make those theories and pathways possible.
