## Introduction

Within APS, life is characterised as:

> viability-oriented, constraint-closed organisation.

APS investigates how such organisation maintains and re-establishes the conditions of its own persistence across change.

As the framework has developed, it has expanded into a series of interconnected research and explanatory pathways addressing persistence, development, adaptation, ecology, evolution, cognition, diagnosis, social organisation, and philosophy of biology. These pathways are not independent research programmes gathered under a common label. Within APS, they are connected by shared questions concerning continuity, viability, living organisation, and organised persistence.

This article functions as a conceptual map of that architecture. Its purpose is not to replace the individual articles that develop particular parts of the framework, but to help readers understand how the major pathways fit together and to identify useful routes through the site depending on their interests and background.

APS is therefore organised not as a collection of isolated topics, but as an integrative analytic framework connecting different questions and explanatory targets across multiple domains of biological inquiry.

> **Where this article fits:** *The Core Structure of APS* provides the canonical account of the framework's conceptual architecture. This article serves a complementary role. Its purpose is to make that architecture visible, helping readers navigate the major pathways, domains, and reading routes that emerge from the wider APS framework.

At the centre of APS lies its account of living organisation. Living systems actively maintain and re-establish conditions relevant to their continued viability while undergoing material, structural, developmental, and environmental change.

APS characterises life as viability-oriented, constraint-closed organisation and treats **organised persistence** as a principal explanatory target: how living organisation maintains continuity through ongoing change.

APS investigates this organisation through three complementary analytic projections:

- **Agency** — what living systems do: viability-oriented organisational activity;

- **Process** — how continuity is maintained despite change through ongoing organisational activity;

- **Scale** — where persistence is organised across the spatial and temporal extents and materially implemented relations relevant to the explanandum.

Agency, Process, and Scale are not separate explanatory modules, biological components, levels, stages, or independent causes. They are analytic projections of the same living organisation, distinguishing different explanatory questions without dividing biological reality.

Their relevance is target-relative. A particular biological explanandum may require one, two, or all three projections, and no projection has intrinsic causal or explanatory priority.

## 1. Persistence and Temporal Organisation

One major pathway within APS develops the temporal organisation of biological persistence.

This pathway explores viability, continuity, adaptation, resilience, temporal organisation, and persistence across time and spatial extent. Within APS, organised persistence directs attention to how living systems maintain continuity through organised transformation rather than by remaining materially or structurally unchanged.

## 2. Development and Organised Continuity

A second major pathway examines how continuity can be investigated through developmental transformation.

Within APS, development provides an important domain for examining organised persistence because growth, repair, adaptation, ageing, and developmental plasticity involve continuing organisation under changing conditions.

## 3. Ecology, Adaptation, and Evolution

APS develops an integrative research pathway connecting questions in ecology, adaptation, and evolution.

Within this framework, ecological questions can be examined in relation to the spatial and temporal organisation of organism–environment persistence; adaptation in relation to the reorganisation of viable continuity under changing conditions; and evolution in relation to the historical transformation of organised persistence across generations.

These are APS formulations of the relevant explanatory targets. Their integration within a common framework does not by itself establish additional explanatory power over established ecological, developmental, or evolutionary explanations.

**inheritance**

**fitness**

This sequence is an APS integrative formulation rather than a claim that these concepts constitute a single causal chain or replace established evolutionary mechanisms and explanations. It organises questions concerning how living organisation capable of persistence is historically transformed through variation, inheritance, selection, and other evolutionary processes.

APS therefore resists reducing evolutionary explanation to any single explanatory factor. Its continuity-oriented formulation is intended to organise relations among biological organisation, development, ecology, inheritance, selection, and historical transformation without treating conceptual integration itself as evidence of comparator-relative explanatory superiority.

## 4. Cognition, Information, and Meaning

APS also develops a cognition-and-mind research pathway.

Within APS, information, meaning, representation, and cognition are investigated in relation to viability-oriented living organisation rather than assumed to be defining properties of life. The framework asks whether and under what conditions increasingly integrated organisation supports cognitive capacities that are not exhausted by immediate or fixed regulation.

## 6. Social Organisation and Distributed Continuity

APS also extends its research programme to questions concerning social organisation.

This pathway investigates how communication, cooperation, institutions, culture, technology, and collective knowledge contribute to forms of continuity distributed across organisms, populations, institutions, and other materially realised social arrangements.

The extension does not require treating societies, institutions, cultures, or other large-scale systems as biological agents. Nor does Scale designate a higher biological level with intrinsic explanatory priority. Scale instead directs attention to the spatial and temporal extents and materially implemented relations relevant to a particular explanandum.

## APS as an Organising Framework

APS is not intended to replace existing biological research programmes or established biological explanations. It provides an integrative analytic framework for investigating questions concerning living organisation, viability, continuity, and organised persistence while remaining compatible with independently established mechanistic, developmental, evolutionary, ecological, physiological, and other biological explanations.

Its principal demonstrated contribution at the present stage is bounded analytic and methodological organisation: clarifying explanatory targets, distinguishing questions, exposing dependencies for assessment, and connecting research pathways without treating conceptual integration itself as evidence of biological truth or explanatory superiority.

Within those limits, APS aims to provide:

- an explicit organisational account of life within the framework;

- a coherent analytic structure for investigating biological organisation;

- ways of relating questions concerning mechanism, function, development, evolution, ecology, cognition, and diagnosis without collapsing their distinct explanatory targets;

- and an integrative architecture through which claims about organised persistence can be formulated and subjected to further comparative and empirical assessment.

Agency, Process, and Scale provide complementary, target-sensitive analytic projections within this architecture. They are not universally necessary dimensions of biological explanation, and no projection has intrinsic causal or explanatory priority.

Organised persistence is a principal APS explanatory target rather than a fourth projection. It identifies the problem of how living organisation maintains continuity through material, structural, developmental, and environmental change. Whether APS provides additional explanatory gain for a particular organised-persistence target must be established by target-matched comparison rather than inferred from the coherence of the framework.

Comparative explanatory methodology remains independently applicable. Its standards can be used to assess APS, neighbouring frameworks, and established biological explanations alike; they do not constitute substantive evidence for APS biological claims.

## How to Use This Map

Readers do not need to follow every pathway in sequence.

Some will arrive through questions about evolution. Others may be interested primarily in development, cognition, diagnosis, ecology, social organisation, or philosophy of biology. The purpose of the architecture map is therefore not to prescribe a single route through APS but to make the structure of the framework visible so that readers can identify pathways most relevant to their interests.

As the framework continues to develop, additional pathways may emerge and existing research pathways may be revised as their individual propositions are assessed.

Within APS, organised persistence remains a principal explanatory target.

Agency, Process, and Scale provide complementary analytic projections through which APS investigates living organisation where those projections are relevant to the explanandum.

The various domains of APS provide research contexts in which questions concerning continuity, organisation, maintenance, transformation, and extension can be investigated without assuming that every domain requires the same explanatory categories or that conceptual integration demonstrates explanatory superiority.

## Key Point

APS is best understood as an integrative analytic framework connecting multiple research and explanatory pathways rather than as a collection of independent topics or a universal explanatory grammar for biology.

Persistence, development, ecology, evolution, cognition, diagnosis, social organisation, and philosophy of biology raise different explanatory questions. APS provides a structured way of relating those questions through its concern with living organisation and organised persistence while preserving their target differences, proposition-specific evidential status, and the independence of established biological explanations.

This architecture map is therefore a guide to the APS research programme: what its major pathways investigate, how they are related within the framework, and where claims require further empirical or comparative assessment.
