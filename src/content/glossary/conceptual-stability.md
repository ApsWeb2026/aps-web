  Conceptual Stability is the condition in which a concept possesses

Conceptual Stability should not be confused with conceptual closure, complete
explanation, scientific consensus, or permanent definition.

Conceptual Closure describes the process through which concepts become
integrated into a coherent explanatory framework. Conceptual Stability
describes the resulting condition in which those concepts can be employed
consistently across explanation, comparison, and further theoretical
development.

Because scientific understanding continues to evolve, Conceptual Stability
does not imply immutability. Stable concepts remain open to refinement,
provided such refinement strengthens rather than fragments the broader
conceptual architecture.

Within APS, Conceptual Stability supports cumulative explanation by
preserving sufficient continuity for dependency pathways, explanatory
grammar, and conceptual relationships to remain coherent as the framework
develops.
