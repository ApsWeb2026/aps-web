## Conventional Framing

Biological systems are often described as adaptive, goal-directed, or purposive.

Such descriptions are frequently interpreted in one of two ways:
- mechanistically, where apparent agency is reduced to causal processes alone
- or cognitively, where agency is treated as requiring representation, intention, or consciousness

Under these framings, agency is either eliminated as explanatory shorthand or restricted to organisms possessing nervous systems and sophisticated cognition.

This leaves the organised purposiveness of living systems without a clear naturalistic grounding.

## The APS Reframing

APS treats biological agency as intrinsic to life itself.

Biological agency is the viability-oriented regulation and modulation of organised activity through which living systems sustain and reorganise the conditions of their own persistence.

Living systems do not merely undergo processes passively. They actively regulate and reorganise their relations with both internal and external conditions relative to viability.

Agency therefore arises from the organisation of living activity itself rather than from externally imposed goals, representations, or intentions.

## Biological Agency and Organisation

Biological agency depends upon viability-oriented organisation.

Living systems maintain themselves through dynamically organised networks of mutually sustaining constraints capable of regulating activity relative to changing conditions.

Metabolism reorganises internal dynamics. Behaviour modifies environmental relations. Physiological systems compensate for perturbation. Development reorganises structure across time.

Agency therefore expresses the active regulation of organised persistence.

APS consequently treats agency not as an additional property layered onto biological systems, but as an expression of viability-oriented, constraint-closed organisation itself.

## Agency and Normativity

Biological agency is intrinsically normative.

Living systems differentiate conditions according to whether they support or undermine continued persistence. Some transformations preserve viability, while others threaten breakdown.

Agency therefore depends upon conditions of success and failure relative to the maintenance of organised persistence.

Normativity is not externally imposed upon living systems. It emerges from the organisation of activity relative to viability.

Agency expresses the active regulation of these viability conditions.

## Agency Is Not Cognition

APS distinguishes biological agency from cognition.

All cognition presupposes biological agency because cognition depends upon systems already capable of viability-oriented regulation.

However, not all agency is cognitive.

Biological systems may regulate and reorganise activity relative to viability without exhibiting the integrated, temporally extended, and semiosis-rich forms of organisation associated with cognition.

Cognition therefore represents a more structured and integrated organisation of agential activity rather than a fundamentally separate category.

## Agency Across Scale

Biological agency operates across spatial and temporal scales.

Cellular organisation regulates metabolic conditions. Organisms regulate behaviour and physiology. Social and ecological interactions may participate in distributed forms of viability-oriented organisation.

These processes are not arranged into hierarchical levels of agency. They form scale-coupled networks of organised activity distributed across space and time.

Agency therefore depends upon organisational integration rather than upon position within a hierarchy.

## Agency and Adaptation

Agency enables adaptation.

Living systems actively modify, reinforce, relax, or reorganise constraints relative to changing conditions. Persistence is therefore maintained not through rigid stability, but through regulated transformation.

Adaptation is one of the principal expressions of biological agency because it reflects the active reorganisation of viability-oriented organisation under changing conditions.

## Agency and Evolution

Biological agency is foundational for evolution.

Evolutionary processes presuppose systems already capable of maintaining and regulating their own persistence. Natural selection does not create agency from non-agency; it transforms already agential forms of organisation across generations.

Evolution therefore operates upon systems capable of viability-oriented regulation.

## Summary

In APS, biological agency is the viability-oriented regulation and modulation of organised activity through which living systems sustain and reorganise the conditions of their own persistence.

Agency is not imposed from outside living systems, nor does it require representation, intention, or consciousness.

It emerges from the organisation of viability-oriented activity itself.

Biological agency therefore grounds:
- adaptation
- normativity
- purposiveness
- semiosis
- and the possibility of cognition

Life does not merely persist.

It actively regulates the conditions of its own persistence.
