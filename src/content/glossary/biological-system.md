A cell, an organ, or a symbiotic association can be studied as a biological system when interactions among its components help explain the phenomenon under investigation. For example, an organ can be delimited to explain a physiological response even when that response depends on resources or signals supplied by the wider organism.

In APS, identifying the system under study and establishing its living status, individuality, or agency are separate steps. Living status concerns whether the system instantiates viability-oriented, constraint-closed organisation. Agency attribution requires evidence of viability-oriented organisational activity in the candidate system. The criteria for individuality depend on the kind of unity or continuity being investigated.

[**What Is a Biological System? — Mechanisms, Agency, and Organised Persistence**](/articles/what-is-a-biological-system/) illustrates these distinctions through a bacterial cell responding to osmotic disturbance and examines how system boundaries are justified in less clear-cut cases.
