Physiological regulation, development, repair, behaviour, and reproduction are all activities performed by biological systems as they maintain the conditions required for continued viability.

Within APS, a biological system is identified by its organisation rather than by any particular material composition. The same explanatory principles apply regardless of the specific structures through which viability is realised.

## Why It Matters

Biology seeks to explain biological systems rather than merely catalogue biological components. Understanding a biological system therefore requires explaining how coordinated activities contribute to the maintenance and re-establishment of viability.

This shifts biological explanation from describing mechanisms in isolation to understanding the organised activity through which living systems persist.

## What It Is Not

A Biological System is not simply any collection of biological parts.

Nor is it defined solely by its chemical composition, genetic material, or constituent mechanisms.

Within APS, a biological system is distinguished by the organisation through which coordinated activities maintain the conditions required for viability.

## Relationship to Other APS Concepts

A Biological System instantiates **Life** through viability-oriented, constraint-closed organisation.

Its activities constitute **Biological Agency**, are organised through **Functional Organisation**, and are continually modified through **Adaptive Reorganisation** to achieve **Organised Persistence** under changing conditions.
