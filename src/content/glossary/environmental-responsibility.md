Environmental Responsibility refers to the obligation-sensitive capacity of agents to evaluate and respond to the effects of their actions on organisms, ecological relations, environmental systems, and future possibilities.

In APS, environmental responsibility emerges when reflective agents become capable of recognising that their actions influence conditions extending beyond their own immediate interests. Responsibility therefore presupposes more than biological significance. It depends upon forms of cognition, evaluation, meaning, and reflective agency that enable concern to extend across wider spatial, temporal, social, and environmental domains.

Environmental responsibility is closely related to power. The greater an agent's capacity to alter environmental conditions, the greater the need to evaluate how those actions affect organisms, ecological relations, environmental systems, and future possibilities. This concern becomes especially important where present actions shape conditions that future organisms, communities, and persons will inherit.

APS currently treats environmental responsibility as more than biological significance but less than a complete environmental ethic. The framework can explain how responsibility emerges from increasingly organised forms of evaluation, but important questions concerning moral standing, environmental justice, collective responsibility, and the transition from value to binding obligation remain areas of ongoing development.

Environmental responsibility therefore serves as a bridge between environmental value and environmental ethics, connecting what matters within the living world to questions concerning how agents ought to act in response.
