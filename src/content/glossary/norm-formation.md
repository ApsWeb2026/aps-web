Norm Formation explains how recurring patterns of Shared Evaluation acquire social continuity.

Not every repeated behaviour becomes a norm. Repetition may result from environmental conditions, incentives, imitation, habit, or coercion. Normativity emerges when socially organised evaluation becomes associated with recognisable expectations concerning how conduct should be interpreted and responded to.

Through Norm Formation, expectations become transmissible across individuals and across time. Departures may be criticised, sanctioned, defended, justified, or used to challenge the norm itself.
