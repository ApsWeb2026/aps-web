Organised persistence is one of the central concepts of APS and provides the framework's primary explanation for how living systems continue through time.

A living system does not persist merely by remaining materially unchanged. On the contrary, living systems undergo continual transformation. Materials are replaced, structures are renewed, developmental trajectories unfold, ecological relationships shift, and environmental conditions change. Despite this continual transformation, living systems maintain a recognisable continuity of organisation. APS refers to this achievement as organised persistence.

In APS, persistence is therefore not understood as passive duration or simple survival. It is the active maintenance of continuity through viability-oriented organisation. Living systems remain viable because they continually regulate internal conditions, repair damage, coordinate activities, adapt to changing circumstances, and sustain the organisational relationships upon which their continued existence depends.

Organised persistence includes processes such as regulation, repair, ecological coupling, developmental transformation, resilience under perturbation, and the ongoing maintenance of viability across time. These activities differ across organisms and across scales of biological organisation, yet they share a common role in preserving continuity despite continual change.

The concept helps distinguish living persistence from the persistence of non-living objects. A stone may persist by remaining relatively unchanged. A living system persists by actively maintaining the conditions of its own continuation. Biological continuity is therefore not a consequence of stability alone but of ongoing organisational activity directed toward viability.

Organised persistence consequently occupies a foundational position within APS. It provides the conceptual bridge connecting agency, viability, process, scale, development, ecology, adaptation, resilience, and evolution. Living systems persist not because change is absent, but because continuity is actively maintained through organised activity across changing conditions and across time.

## Key Point

In APS, organised persistence is the viability-oriented continuity of living organisation maintained through ongoing activity across material, developmental, ecological, and temporal change.
