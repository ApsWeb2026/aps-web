Survival is the continued existence of a living organism. In ordinary biological language, organisms survive when they remain alive despite the many internal and external conditions that threaten their continued functioning.

Within the Agency–Process–Scale (APS) framework, however, survival is not treated as a primary explanatory concept. Rather, it is understood as the outcome of successful viability-oriented, constraint-closed organisation. Survival describes what has been achieved, but it does not explain how that achievement is continuously maintained.

APS therefore distinguishes survival from the organisational activities through which living systems maintain themselves.

Biological Agency is the viability-oriented organisational activity through which living systems actively maintain and re-establish the conditions of their own persistence.

Operational Persistence refers to the ongoing organisational accomplishment through which an organism continuously maintains those conditions despite constant internal and external change.

Organised Persistence refers to the enduring continuity achieved through this ongoing organisational activity and identifies the principal explanatory target of biological explanation.

From this perspective, survival is not an independent organising principle or biological cause. Living systems do not persist because they are driven by survival itself. Rather, they survive because their agency continuously modulates the constraints that maintain viability. Survival therefore names the successful outcome of living organisation rather than the organisational principle that explains it.

## APS Clarification

APS does not reject the concept of survival. Instead, it argues that survival is a descriptive term identifying the continued existence of an organism, whereas biological explanation seeks to understand the organisation that makes such continued existence possible. APS therefore gives explanatory priority to viability, biological agency, operational persistence, and organised persistence rather than to survival itself.
