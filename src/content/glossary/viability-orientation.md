## Definition (In Brief)
Viability-orientation is the organisation of a system such that its processes contribute to maintaining the conditions required for its continued existence.

## APS Clarification
Viability-orientation does not imply intention, foresight, or representation. It refers to the structured relation between the processes of a system and the conditions under which that system persists.

In APS, viability is not defined by reference to life itself, but in terms of the persistence of an organised system within a bounded region of possible states. A system is therefore not viability-oriented simply because it persists, but because its internal organisation is such that its processes are systematically related to the maintenance of those persistence conditions.

This relation may be direct (e.g. metabolic processes maintaining internal stability) or indirect (e.g. behavioural processes altering environmental conditions). What matters is not the specific mechanism, but the organisation of processes relative to the conditions of continued existence.

## Key Point
A system is not living because it persists; it is living because its organisation is such that its processes are systematically related to maintaining the conditions under which it can persist.
