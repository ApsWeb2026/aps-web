## Conventional Framing

Fitness is commonly understood as reproductive success or the capacity of organisms to contribute genes to future generations. Within evolutionary biology, fitness is often measured through patterns of survival, reproduction, and differential contribution to future populations. These approaches provide important tools for understanding evolutionary change and for modelling how populations transform through time. At the same time, fitness is frequently interpreted as a property of isolated traits, a measure of optimal design, or a ranking of biological superiority. APS rejects these stronger interpretations because fitness cannot be understood independently of the organisational and ecological conditions under which living systems persist.

## The APS Reframing

In APS, fitness is the historically situated capacity of persistence-sustaining organisation to contribute to continuity across generations under changing developmental and ecological conditions. Fitness therefore concerns the differential continuity of organised persistence rather than reproductive output alone. Living systems maintain themselves through ongoing viability-oriented activity distributed across physiology, development, behaviour, ecological interaction, and environmental coupling. Because continuity depends upon the successful organisation of these activities, fitness cannot be reduced to isolated traits, genes, or static properties. Fitness emerges through the capacity of organised living systems to remain sufficiently viable for continuity to occur through time.

APS therefore approaches fitness as a relational and context-dependent concept. Organisms are not fit in abstraction from their developmental histories, ecological circumstances, or organisational capacities. Fitness reflects the historical continuity of organised persistence under particular conditions rather than conformity to any universal standard of optimisation. This perspective also clarifies the relationship between viability and fitness. Viability concerns the conditions under which organised persistence succeeds or fails in the present, whereas fitness concerns the longer-term continuity of such persistence across generations. Living systems must therefore be viable before they can be fit. Fitness presupposes organised persistence rather than explaining its original emergence.

> **Where this concept fits:** Fitness functions as a bridge connecting viability, adaptation, inheritance, natural selection, and evolution. It helps explain how differences in organised persistence contribute to differential continuity across generations and therefore occupies a central position within the APS account of evolutionary transformation.

## Fitness in APS

Fitness contributes to evolutionary explanation by helping account for why some forms of organised persistence remain historically continuous while others do not. Adaptation contributes to fitness by reorganising living systems under changing conditions. Natural selection acts upon differences in fitness by differentially stabilising forms of persistence-sustaining organisation that contribute more effectively to continuity. Evolution then transforms such organisation across longer historical timescales. Fitness therefore links viability in the present to continuity across generations and provides one of the principal ways through which APS connects biological organisation to evolutionary transformation.

From the APS perspective, fitness is best understood as differential continuity rather than optimisation. It does not measure perfection, superiority, or ideal design. Instead, it reflects the historically situated capacity of organised living systems to maintain continuity under changing developmental and ecological conditions. Fitness thus links viability, persistence, adaptation, inheritance, selection, and evolution within a unified framework of organised biological persistence. It occupies a central role within APS because it helps explain how continuity-producing organisation contributes to the historical transformation of life.

## Summary

In APS, fitness is the historically situated capacity of persistence-sustaining organisation to contribute to continuity across generations under changing developmental and ecological conditions. Fitness is not adequately understood as reproductive output, optimisation, or biological superiority alone. Rather, it concerns the differential continuity of organised persistence through time. APS therefore approaches fitness as a context-dependent property of organised living systems and as a key concept linking viability, adaptation, inheritance, natural selection, and evolution within the broader framework of organised biological persistence.
