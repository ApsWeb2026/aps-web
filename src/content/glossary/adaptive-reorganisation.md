## Definition

Adaptive Reorganisation is the continual adjustment of functional relationships through which a living system maintains or re-establishes the organisational conditions required for viability under changing internal or environmental circumstances.

## In Brief

Living systems maintain themselves not by preserving fixed patterns of organisation but by continually reorganising their functional activity as circumstances change.

Physiological regulation, repair, development, behavioural adjustment, and acclimation all involve adaptive changes in the coordination of biological functions while preserving the viability-oriented organisation of the organism.

Adaptive Reorganisation therefore explains how organisational continuity is actively maintained through ongoing organisational change.

## Why It Matters

Adaptive Reorganisation explains why biological stability and biological change are complementary rather than opposing characteristics of living systems.

The persistence of life depends not upon resisting change but upon continually reorganising functional activity in ways that preserve viability.

This provides an organisational account of biological flexibility without abandoning continuity of identity.

## What It Is Not

Adaptive Reorganisation is not simply adaptation in the evolutionary sense.

Nor is it merely the replacement of one mechanism by another.

It concerns the coordinated reorganisation of multiple interdependent functional relationships through which living systems preserve their continuing organisation.

## Relationship to Other APS Concepts

Adaptive Reorganisation depends upon **Functional Organisation** and frequently employs **Functional Equivalence** as different mechanisms come to realise continuing organisational roles.

Through continual reorganisation, living systems achieve **Operational Persistence** despite changing internal and environmental conditions.
