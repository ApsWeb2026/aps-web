## Definition

Functional Organisation is the coordinated arrangement and ongoing integration of viability-oriented biological functions through which a living system maintains and continually re-establishes the conditions of its own persistence.

## In Brief

Within APS, biological functions do not operate independently. Each function derives its biological significance from its contribution to the wider organisation of the living system and from its dependence upon other functions within that organisation.

Functional Organisation therefore refers to the coordinated activity through which biological functions collectively realise the continuing organisation of life. It explains how diverse functions become integrated into a coherent viability-oriented whole rather than remaining isolated physiological or behavioural activities.

Because living systems continually undergo material turnover, developmental change, and environmental variation, Functional Organisation is not a fixed arrangement of parts. It is an ongoing organisational activity through which functional relationships are continually enacted, adjusted, and maintained.

## Why It Matters

Functional Organisation explains why biological explanation cannot stop with identifying individual functions or the mechanisms that realise them.

Knowing what a particular function contributes does not by itself explain how living systems persist. Persistence depends upon the coordinated organisation of many mutually dependent functions whose integrated activity continually maintains the viability of the organism.

Functional Organisation therefore provides the organisational framework within which biological functions acquire their significance.

## What It Is Not

Functional Organisation is not simply the presence of many functions within the same organism.

It is not a catalogue of physiological activities, nor merely the structural arrangement of biological components.

Nor is it identical with mechanism. Mechanisms materially realise biological functions, but Functional Organisation concerns the coordinated relationships through which those functions collectively contribute to organised persistence.

## Relationship to Other APS Concepts

Functional Organisation builds upon **Biological Function** by explaining how individual functions become integrated within the continuing activity of living systems.

It provides the foundation for **Functional Equivalence**, which explains how organisational roles can remain stable despite changes in their mechanistic realisation.

It also underpins **Adaptive Reorganisation**, through which organisms continually adjust functional relationships under changing conditions.

Together these concepts contribute to **Operational Persistence**, the continuing organisational accomplishment through which living systems maintain viability-oriented organised persistence.
