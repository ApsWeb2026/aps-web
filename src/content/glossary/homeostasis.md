## Conventional framing

Homeostasis concerns the relative stability that physiological activity sustains. Claude Bernard’s account of the *milieu intérieur*, or internal environment, made the conditions in which tissues function a subject of experimental inquiry. Walter Cannon used *homeostasis* to describe the relative steadiness maintained by coordinated physiological activity. [How Did Physiologists Explain Internal Stability?](/articles/how-did-physiologists-explain-internal-stability/) traces the experiments that helped establish this account.

A homeostatically regulated variable can fluctuate. Blood glucose normally rises after a meal and later falls as the balance between the supply and uptake of glucose changes. Conversely, a nearly steady value can conceal substantial changes in the processes that maintain it. Establishing that a condition is regulated requires evidence about the activity that keeps it within workable limits or brings it back within them.

Negative feedback is one way of sustaining homeostasis. A change elicits a response whose effects limit the departure. Homeostasis, allostasis, and homeorhesis have overlapping explanatory emphases. Homeostasis concerns the relative stability of internal conditions. Allostasis draws attention to adjustments in regulation as demands change, including anticipatory adjustments. Homeorhesis concerns the regulation of an unfolding biological trajectory. The same physiological processes can be relevant to more than one of these questions.

## APS reframing

APS treats homeostasis as one way physiological regulation can contribute to organised persistence. The explanatory task is to identify which condition is stabilised, which processes sustain it, and how maintaining that condition contributes to the continued functioning of the system at the scale under investigation.

Glucose regulation illustrates two related questions. The first asks how coordinated activity limits a departure in blood glucose. The second asks how the organism maintains the tissues, circulation, and signalling on which that response depends. An account of constraint closure requires evidence of reciprocal dependence among the constraints involved in sustaining the organisation. Evidence of a homeostatic response establishes only part of that account.

Homeostasis contributes to organised persistence where continued functioning depends on relative stability. Living systems also persist through changes in their organisation and in the conditions they can sustain. The relevance of homeostasis therefore depends on what must remain relatively stable, over what interval, and for which activities to continue.

## Key Point

Homeostasis is an achievement of ongoing physiological activity. Explaining it requires showing what stays relatively stable, how that stability is maintained, and why it matters to the living system.
