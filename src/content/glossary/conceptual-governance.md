Conceptual Governance distinguishes disciplined conceptual development from
unrestricted conceptual change. Scientific concepts evolve as understanding
improves, but conceptual revision should strengthen rather than weaken the
coherence of the explanatory framework.

Within APS, Conceptual Governance is implemented through mechanisms such as
canonical definitions, canonical lock dates, revision histories, glossary
audits, conceptual audits, and dependency audits. These mechanisms ensure
that conceptual changes are evaluated in relation to their consequences for
the wider conceptual architecture rather than being introduced in isolation.

Conceptual Governance therefore complements Conceptual Stability.
Conceptual Stability describes the condition in which concepts function
reliably within explanation, whereas Conceptual Governance describes the
organisational processes that establish, preserve, and responsibly develop
that stability over time.

In this way, Conceptual Governance enables APS to remain scientifically
responsive while maintaining the conceptual continuity required for
cumulative biological explanation.
