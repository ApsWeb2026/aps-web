## What is a biological individual?

In APS, a biological individual is a **unit of viability-oriented, constraint-closed organisation**. It is a system that actively maintains the conditions required for its own persistence.

This definition departs from views that identify individuals primarily in terms of:
- physical boundaries  
- genetic homogeneity  
- developmental origin  
- or morphological unity  

While such features may correlate with individuality, they do not define it. What defines an individual is its organisational capacity to sustain viability through coordinated, self-maintaining activity.

A biological individual is therefore not fundamentally a static object but an organised process of persistence.

## Individuals as loci of agency

Biological individuals are the primary loci of biological agency.

Agency in APS refers to the ongoing modulation and regulation of activity relative to viability conditions. This activity is not distributed arbitrarily across classificatory categories such as species or taxa; it is enacted by organised systems capable of sustaining themselves.

Individuals therefore:
- sustain themselves through coordinated internal processes  
- regulate exchanges with their environment  
- maintain constraint-closed organisation across time  
- and reorganise activity under changing conditions  

Agency belongs to individuals in this organisational sense rather than to abstract groupings or descriptive categories.

## Individuals, organisms, and agents

APS distinguishes between biological individuals, organisms, and agents while recognising that these concepts frequently overlap.

A **biological individual** is a unit of viability-oriented organisation.

An **organism** is a biologically integrated living system exhibiting sufficiently coordinated and persistent organisation to function as a coherent living whole.

A **biological agent** is a system capable of viability-oriented regulation and modulation of activity.

In many familiar cases — such as bacterial cells, plants, or animals — the same system is simultaneously:
- an organism  
- a biological individual  
- and a biological agent  

However, these concepts are not identical.

Some systems may exhibit partial agency without full organismal integration, while some collective systems may display degrees of individuality without constituting single organisms in the traditional sense. Conversely, classificatory entities such as species or taxa are neither organisms nor agents, because they do not actively regulate and sustain themselves as organised systems.

APS therefore treats these concepts as analytically distinct but organisationally related.

## Individuation and organisation

Individuation in APS is grounded in organisation rather than static structure.

A biological individual is individuated when its processes:
- are sufficiently integrated  
- participate in a shared regime of constraint closure  
- and sustain a coherent trajectory of organised persistence  

This allows individuality to be identified across a range of biological cases, including:
- single cells  
- multicellular organisms  
- symbiotic systems where organisation is sufficiently integrated  
- and certain collective systems exhibiting coordinated persistence  

Individuation is therefore not tied to a particular morphology or privileged level of organisation. What matters is the viability-oriented integration of processes capable of sustaining organised persistence.

## Individuals, species, and taxa

APS distinguishes clearly between biological individuals and classificatory categories.

- **Biological individuals** are organised systems capable of sustaining viability  
- **Species** are patterns of organised persistence distributed across populations and generations  
- **Taxa** are classificatory groupings tracking similarities and continuities across such patterns  

Species and taxa do not themselves regulate, maintain, or sustain viability-oriented organisation. They describe recurring organisational continuities across biological individuals.

This distinction prevents explanatory confusion in which causation or agency is attributed to categories rather than to organised systems.

## Biological vs processual individuals

APS further distinguishes between biological individuals and processual individuals.

- A **biological individual** refers to an organised unit at a given time  
- A **processual individual** refers to that organised unit understood through its continuity across time  

This distinction allows APS to:
- identify the locus of agency and organisation  
- while also explaining continuity despite material turnover, developmental change, and adaptive reorganisation  

Identity therefore becomes a matter of organised persistence rather than static material sameness.

## Individuals across scale

Biological individuality is scale-sensitive but not hierarchically fixed.

At smaller scales, individuality may correspond to cellular systems. At larger scales, it may correspond to multicellular organisms, symbiotic associations, or highly integrated collectives. What matters is not the scale itself, but whether the system exhibits sufficiently integrated, viability-oriented, constraint-closed organisation.

APS therefore rejects the idea that individuality belongs exclusively to a single privileged level of biological organisation.

Individuality instead emerges wherever organised persistence becomes sufficiently integrated to sustain viability across time.

## Why individuality matters

Clarifying biological individuality has several important consequences.

It:
- identifies the primary locus of biological agency and persistence  
- distinguishes organisation from classification  
- prevents explanatory reification of species and taxa  
- clarifies the organisational basis of adaptation and evolution  
- and provides criteria for analysing borderline or collective systems  

Without a coherent account of individuality, biological explanation risks confusing descriptive categories with the organised systems through which living persistence is actually enacted.

## Key Point

In APS, a biological individual is a unit of viability-oriented, constraint-closed organisation and a primary locus of biological agency. Individuals, organisms, and agents frequently overlap, but they are analytically distinct concepts grounded in different aspects of organised biological persistence.
