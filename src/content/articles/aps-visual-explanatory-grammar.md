# APS Visual Explanatory Grammar

## Illustration Policy and Diagram Architecture Standards for APS_WEB

APS diagrams are not decorative illustrations. They are explanatory instruments designed to assist the interpretation, organisation, and navigation of biological explanation within the APS framework.

This document establishes the principles governing the construction, interpretation, and integration of diagrams across APS_WEB.

The aim is to ensure that APS figures remain:
- explanatory rather than decorative,
- pedagogically intelligible,
- visually coherent,
- and structurally consistent across the framework.

APS diagrams should therefore be understood as:
- analytic projections of organised persistence,
- architectural representations of explanatory structure,
- and pedagogical aids for navigating biological explanation.

---

# 1. The Purpose of APS Diagrams

APS diagrams exist to:
- clarify relations within viability-oriented organised persistence,
- stabilise explanatory orientation,
- reduce conceptual ambiguity,
- visualise organisational continuity,
- and support progressive reader understanding across multiple explanatory domains.

Diagrams do not replace prose explanation. They support and stabilise it.

The governing principle is:

> APS diagrams should clarify explanatory organisation without overwhelming the reader with conceptual density.

APS diagrams should therefore prioritise:
- intelligibility,
- hierarchy,
- orientation,
- and explanatory clarity.

---

# 2. Diagram Types and Pedagogical Levels

No single diagram should attempt to perform every explanatory function simultaneously.

APS diagrams operate at multiple levels of explanatory complexity.

---

## Level 1 — Orientation Diagrams

### Purpose
- Reader onboarding
- Homepage integration
- Initial conceptual orientation
- Framework identity

### Characteristics
- Minimal terminology
- Strong visual hierarchy
- Low conceptual density
- Immediate readability
- Limited node count
- Large spacing
- Clear domain labels

### Typical Uses
- Homepage diagrams
- Introductory orientation pages
- Overview sections

### Examples
- Agency–Process–Scale
- Basic viability architecture
- Minimal explanatory domain maps

### Governing Principle

Orientation diagrams should answer:

> “What is APS?”

not:

> “What is the complete internal architecture of APS?”

---

## Level 2 — Explanatory Architecture Diagrams

### Purpose
- Conceptual integration
- Structural clarification
- Intermediate explanatory synthesis

### Characteristics
- Moderate conceptual density
- Organised domain structure
- Explicit explanatory relations
- Controlled terminology
- Strong caption support

### Typical Uses
- Orientation articles
- Structural explanation pages
- Comparative articles
- Domain integration pages

### Examples
- APS Architecture Map
- Constitutional integration diagrams
- Evolutionary explanation diagrams

---

## Level 3 — Canonical Synthesis Diagrams

### Purpose
- Deep explanatory integration
- Advanced conceptual architecture
- Multi-domain continuity mapping

### Characteristics
- High conceptual density
- Multi-layer relations
- Full architectural integration
- Extensive cross-domain structure

### Typical Uses
- Dedicated architecture pages
- Advanced conceptual articles
- Canonical synthesis documents

### Restriction

Canonical synthesis diagrams should not normally appear on:
- the homepage,
- introductory orientation pages,
- or reader entry pathways.

---

# 3. Visual Semantics

APS diagrams should employ consistent visual semantics across the site so that readers gradually learn how to interpret diagrammatic structure.

---

# 3.1 Colour Semantics

Colours should indicate broad explanatory domains rather than decorative variation.

## Preliminary Semantic Structure

| Colour Domain | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Blue | Constitutional organisation |
| Green | Evolutionary continuity |
| Amber | Cognition and evaluation |
| Red | Diagnostic perturbation or failure |
| Teal | Ecological coupling |
| Grey | Explanatory integration / meta-level structure |

## Governing Principle

Colour should:
- orient,
- group,
- and stabilise interpretation,

not:
- create visual spectacle.

Muted palettes are preferred over saturated palettes.

---

# 3.2 Arrow Semantics

Arrows must carry stable explanatory meaning.

## Solid arrows
Primary explanatory dependence or continuity.

## Dashed arrows
Indirect or conditional relations.

## Bidirectional arrows
Reciprocal organisational dependence.

## Loop arrows
Feedback, recursive regulation, or persistence dynamics.

## Governing Principle

Arrows should not ambiguously mix:
- causation,
- temporal succession,
- conceptual association,
- and explanatory dependence.

Where necessary, captions should clarify arrow meaning explicitly.

---

# 3.3 Shape Semantics

Shapes should also remain consistent across APS diagrams.

## Rounded rectangles
Stable conceptual domains.

## Circles
Processes or dynamic relations.

## Diamonds
Transitions, distinctions, or diagnostic bifurcations.

## Shaded regions
Integrated explanatory clusters.

## Governing Principle

Shape variation should indicate explanatory role rather than decorative variation.

---

# 4. Diagram Construction Standards

---

## 4.1 Canonical File Format

SVG is the canonical APS diagram format.

SVG is preferred because it supports:
- scalability,
- editability,
- typography control,
- semantic layering,
- and responsive web integration.

Raster exports should be generated only from canonical SVG masters.

---

## 4.2 Typography

APS diagrams should employ clean sans-serif typography with strong visual hierarchy.

### Hierarchy Rules
- Titles larger than domain labels
- Domain labels larger than node labels
- Captions visually subordinate to diagrams

Typography should prioritise:
- readability,
- hierarchy,
- and interpretive scanning.

---

## 4.3 Spacing

APS diagrams should favour:
- generous whitespace,
- domain separation,
- and uncluttered geometry.

A smaller number of highly legible concepts is preferable to dense conceptual saturation.

Compression should be avoided whenever possible.

---

## 4.4 Diagram Titles

Every major APS diagram should include:
- a concise title,
- or surrounding page heading identifying the figure’s explanatory purpose.

---

## 4.5 APS Figure Identification

Major diagrams may use APS figure identifiers such as:
- APS-F1
- APS-F2
- APS-C1
- APS-E1

This system may later support:
- publication integration,
- cross-referencing,
- and reusable figure architecture.

---

# 5. Caption and Interpretation Standards

Captions are interpretive components of APS diagrams, not merely labels.

APS captions should typically contain three functions.

---

## 5.1 Structural Identification

What the figure shows.

### Example
> “A simplified orientation map of major explanatory domains within APS.”

---

## 5.2 Interpretive Clarification

What relations or symbols represent.

### Example
> “Arrows indicate relations of explanatory continuity and dependence.”

---

## 5.3 Conceptual Framing

Why the structure matters.

### Example
> “The figure illustrates how multiple domains of biological explanation are integrated within viability-oriented organised persistence.”

---

## Governing Principle

Captions should reduce interpretive ambiguity before readers encounter diagrammatic complexity.

---

# 6. Cross-Site Diagram Integration Policy

APS diagrams must be integrated according to pedagogical role and reader context.

---

## 6.1 Homepage Restrictions

Homepage diagrams should:
- remain visually restrained,
- avoid excessive conceptual density,
- support orientation rather than deep synthesis,
- and function primarily as conceptual anchors.

The homepage should communicate:
- APS identity,

not:
- total architectural complexity.

---

## 6.2 One Major Diagram Per Page

As a general rule:
- one major explanatory diagram per page is preferred.

Multiple dense diagrams on a single page often:
- fragment explanatory attention,
- overload interpretation,
- and weaken conceptual pacing.

---

## 6.3 Progressive Disclosure

Diagram complexity should increase progressively across the site.

### Early orientation
Simple diagrams.

### Intermediate explanation
Moderate integration diagrams.

### Advanced architecture
Full synthesis diagrams.

Readers should learn the visual explanatory grammar gradually.

---

## 6.4 Diagram Migration Principle

When a diagram becomes too dense for a page’s pedagogical role, it should migrate to:
- a dedicated architecture page,
rather than forcing additional complexity into the original page.

---

# 7. Governing Principles

APS diagrams should remain:
- explanatory rather than decorative,
- organisational rather than merely classificatory,
- pedagogically staged,
- visually coherent,
- and conceptually integrated.

The central aim of APS visual architecture is not graphical sophistication for its own sake, but explanatory intelligibility.

The governing visual principle of APS is therefore:

> Diagrams should reveal organisational structure while preserving interpretive clarity.
