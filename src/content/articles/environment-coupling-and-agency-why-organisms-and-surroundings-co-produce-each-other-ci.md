## Introduction

> **Where this article fits:** The preceding ecology articles explain why organisms and environments form continuity-producing systems and how organism–environment coupling sustains viable persistence. This article explains how biological agency operates within those systems. APS argues that agency is not exercised independently of organism–environment coupling but through the continual regulation and reorganisation of the ecological relations that sustain life.

Biological agency is often understood as the capacity of organisms to act upon their environments.

Although this captures an important aspect of living systems, it can also encourage the misleading impression that agency originates entirely within organisms before being directed outward toward an external world.

APS proposes a different starting point.

Living systems are never agents independently of the ecological relations through which they persist. Their capacity to regulate, reorganise, and sustain viability depends continuously upon organism–environment coupling. Agency therefore does not stand apart from ecological organisation but operates through it.

This perspective shifts attention from organisms acting upon environments to organisms continually regulating the continuity relations that make persistence possible. Biological agency is therefore neither internal control nor passive responsiveness. It is the ongoing viability-oriented activity through which living systems maintain and reorganise their ecological conditions of existence.

## Agency Requires Coupling

Agency cannot be separated from organism–environment coupling.

Living systems sustain themselves only through continual exchanges of energy, materials, information, and constraint with their surroundings. These exchanges are not incidental interactions occurring after organisms have been constituted. They are the continuity-producing relations through which viable organisation is maintained.

Agency therefore presupposes ecological coupling.

Every act of regulation modifies the relations linking organisms with the conditions upon which persistence depends. Behaviour alters environmental engagement, metabolism reorganises energetic exchange, development reshapes organism–environment relations, and adaptive activity modifies the opportunities available for future persistence.

Agency is consequently expressed through the continual regulation of ecological continuity rather than through isolated internal processes.

## Agency Regulates Organism–Environment Relations

If coupling provides the continuity through which living systems persist, agency provides the activity through which that continuity is continually regulated.

Organisms do not simply experience changing environments. They actively reorganise their relations with those environments in ways that preserve viability. They alter patterns of behaviour, redistribute physiological activity, modify ecological interactions, and exploit new opportunities as conditions change.

The significance of these activities lies not in environmental modification alone but in the maintenance of organised persistence. Agency continually evaluates and reorganises organism–environment relations relative to viability, allowing persistence to continue despite changing ecological circumstances.

APS therefore understands biological agency as the active regulation of organism–environment coupling. Rather than acting independently of ecological organisation, agency operates through the continual maintenance and reorganisation of the continuity relations that sustain living systems.

## Agency Reshapes Ecological Conditions

Agency is expressed not only through the regulation of existing organism–environment relations but also through their continual reorganisation.

Living systems modify the ecological conditions supporting their own persistence through metabolism, behaviour, development, niche construction, and countless other forms of biological activity. These changes do not simply alter external surroundings. They reorganise the continuity relations through which future viability becomes possible.

This reciprocal organisation explains why organisms and environments are best understood as co-producing one another. Organisms continually reshape the ecological conditions under which they persist, while those changing conditions simultaneously reshape the organisation, behaviour, and developmental possibilities of the organisms themselves.

Agency therefore contributes directly to the ongoing production of ecological organisation. Persistence is maintained not by preserving a fixed relationship between organism and environment, but by continually reorganising that relationship as ecological conditions change.

## Agency Maintains Ecological Continuity

The ultimate significance of biological agency lies in its contribution to continuity.

Living systems survive because they continually regulate the organism–environment relations upon which viability depends. Physiological adjustment, behavioural flexibility, developmental plasticity, ecological modification, and adaptive reorganisation all contribute to preserving continuity under changing conditions.

Agency therefore operates through the ongoing regulation of ecological continuity rather than through isolated acts of control. Its activity is directed toward sustaining organised persistence by reorganising the continuity relations linking organisms and their environments.

This perspective also clarifies the relationship between agency and resilience. Resilience describes the capacity of persistence systems to reorganise following perturbation, while agency provides the viability-oriented activity through which that reorganisation is achieved. Ecological continuity is therefore maintained through the continual operation of biological agency.

## Why Agency Is Not Internal Control

Understanding agency in this way avoids a common misunderstanding.

Agency is sometimes imagined as an internal controller directing behaviour toward an otherwise passive environment. APS rejects this picture because it separates the organism from the ecological relations through which persistence becomes possible.

Agency is instead distributed across the ongoing organisation of organism–environment coupling. Living systems regulate viability by participating in ecological continuity rather than by imposing control upon an independent external world.

This understanding prepares the way for a broader APS account of organism–world coupling. Agency does not consist in mastering environmental conditions but in participating within the organised continuity through which living systems and their environments continually co-produce one another.

## Conclusion

APS explains biological agency as the viability-oriented regulation of organism–environment coupling.

Living systems remain viable because they continually reorganise the ecological relations through which persistence is sustained. Behaviour, metabolism, development, adaptation, and ecological modification are therefore expressions of agency because they regulate the continuity linking organisms with the conditions of their own existence.

Agency is consequently neither an internal controller nor a property exercised independently of ecological organisation. It is the ongoing activity through which organism–environment coupling is regulated in the service of viable persistence.

This ecological understanding of agency completes the transition from the ecology domain to the broader APS account of biological agency, where the emphasis shifts from ecological continuity to the viability-oriented activity through which that continuity is produced and maintained.
