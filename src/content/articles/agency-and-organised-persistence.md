## Introduction

An organism can remain continuous as its material constituents turn over, its structures are remodelled, and its developmental and environmental conditions change. Repair can replace damaged structures with arrangements that differ in detail, and development can transform the organisation of the individual while preserving its continuity. These cases raise a common question. What remains continuous, and what enables that continuity through change?

APS calls the phenomenon to be explained **organised persistence**. It uses **biological agency** to identify the viability-oriented activity of living organisation (Spencer, 2026). Their explanatory connection depends on showing how particular activities make a material difference to that continuity.

## Agency and Organised Persistence

APS understands life as viability-oriented, constraint-closed organisation. Biological agency identifies the activity of that organisation insofar as it is oriented towards viability. Organised persistence concerns how the organisation continues through changes in its components, activities, and relations.

Agency and organised persistence are analytically distinguishable aspects of the same living organisation. Their relation is neither one of causal succession nor one in which agency acts as an independent cause. A causal explanation can show how a particular regulatory, reparative, or compensatory activity changes the conditions under which the organisation continues. It must also account for the other processes and conditions on which continuity depends.

Consider a tissue that remains functional after damage. Its continuity may depend on how much organised structure survives the injury, on the availability of resources, and on repair or compensation that restores function. An agency analysis directs attention to the viability-oriented activity involved. The explanation must specify what the surviving structure, surrounding conditions, and repair processes each contribute.

## Maintenance and Re-establishment Through Change

Maintenance and re-establishment describe different demands on living organisation. Regulation can maintain conditions within a viable range despite ordinary fluctuations. More extensive disruption can require the restoration of a lost relation, the redistribution of functional contributions, or a reorganisation that sustains viability under changed conditions. Evidence that a system maintains a condition during ordinary functioning may tell us little about how it recovers once the processes that maintained that condition are themselves impaired.

This is where the distinction from homeostatic regulation matters. Continuity can depend on keeping variables within a workable range, and it can also depend on changing the regulatory or functional relations that made the previous range sustainable. Recovery may therefore involve establishing a new organisation of activity through which viable conditions can be maintained.

Repair and development show how continuity can depend on transformation. A damaged tissue can recover function through an arrangement that differs from its previous one. During development, structures, physiological relations, and patterns of activity can change substantially while the same individual continues. The explanatory task is to identify the continuity relevant to the case, establish which transformations are compatible with it, and determine where viability-oriented activity contributes. In a developmental case, that contribution might involve regulating a transition or compensating for a disruption. Explaining the developmental trajectory also requires an account of the processes through which the changes occur.

Cryptobiosis provides a limiting case. In some forms of cryptobiosis, including states induced by severe dehydration, metabolic activity can become difficult to detect while the organism retains the capacity to resume activity under permissive conditions (Clegg, 2001). The empirical evidence concerns what can be detected during the interval and what resumes afterwards. In APS terms, the case illustrates how organised persistence can span an interval during which viability-oriented activity is temporarily unexpressed, provided that the organisation enabling its resumption remains materially continuous. This interpretation leaves open which activities prepare the system for the interval, which conditions preserve it, and which processes enable recovery.

## Constraints and the Organisation of Activity

Material processes in living systems are organised through constraints that enable, channel, and limit what can occur. In organisational accounts of biological autonomy, constraints can depend on processes that they in turn help sustain (Moreno and Mossio, 2015). This helps specify how viability-oriented activity is realised. A regulatory relation can channel a response to perturbation, while a structural relation can make repair possible by preserving conditions needed for the activities involved. Reorganisation can alter those relations when existing arrangements cease to sustain viability.

Where an organism modifies a constraint, the explanation must identify the processes that make the modification possible and show how it affects the activity relevant to continuity. Agency describes the viability-oriented character of that activity. The causal work is carried out by the materially realised processes and relations.

## Viability, Normativity, and Biological Evaluation

Conditions differ in their consequences for living systems. A perturbation can be harmless under one set of circumstances and destructive under another, depending on the organisation’s current state and its capacity to respond. APS uses **viability-relative normativity** for this asymmetry. Accounts of biological autonomy and adaptivity similarly examine how living systems regulate their activity in relation to conditions that bear on their continued viability (Di Paolo, 2005).

In APS, **biological evaluation is the process through which agency generates significance**. A condition’s consequences for continuity and the significance it acquires in the modulation of activity raise distinguishable questions. For example, a change can threaten viability, while the organisation of the system determines whether and how that change becomes significant for its activity. Where evaluation bears on persistence, the task is to show how it contributes to the modulation of activity and how that activity in turn affects continuity.

## Organism–Environment Relations and Scale

The conditions of living continuity include relations with the environment. Resource availability, temperature, chemical conditions, and interactions with other organisms can affect whether viable organisation continues. Organisms can also modify some of those conditions through their activity. A persistence explanation may therefore need to establish both which environmental relations affect viability and how the organism’s activity sustains or changes those relations.

Scale helps specify the spatial and temporal extents over which relevant processes and dependencies are organised. A wider extent can matter because exchanges, constraints, or interactions across it affect the organisation whose continuity is being explained. Whether agency should be attributed to any wider organisation is a further question that depends on the grounds for attribution in that case.

An activity occurs at a particular time within an organisation shaped by its history, and its effects may extend beyond the period in which it is performed. Repair can leave a structure that supports later functioning, and regulation can prevent damage that would otherwise compromise subsequent recovery. Establishing those effects can help explain continuity within a lifespan. Explaining historical transformation across generations requires additional evidence about the processes responsible for that transformation.

## Assessing the Explanatory Contribution of Agency

Whether agency warrants explanatory priority depends on the question. A physiological, developmental, or ecological explanation may already identify the dependencies that sustain the continuity at issue. An agency analysis can still orient inquiry by drawing attention to how viable conditions are maintained or re-established. Its explanatory contribution becomes assessable when it specifies a dependency, a distinction, or an inference that bears on the explanandum.

A claim of explanatory gain carries a further burden. Specify the persistence target, reconstruct the strongest relevant comparator, and state both accounts in terms that make their explanatory commitments comparable. If a substantive difference survives that comparison, identify what observation, intervention, counterfactual analysis, or other evidence could establish its consequences for the target. For example, an agency analysis might identify a dependence of recovery on the coordination of repair and regulation. Establishing gain would require evidence that this dependence affects recovery and that accounting for it extends what the comparator can explain.

If an adequate comparison shows that the proposed contribution is already available from the comparator or adds no explanatory capacity for the target, the warranted verdict is **no additional gain**. If a potentially consequential difference remains and the available evidence cannot settle its contribution, the verdict is **unresolved**. A clearer formulation or a better connection between existing accounts can still have value. Claims about that value require evidence appropriate to the achievement.

## Conclusion

Living organisation can remain continuous through material turnover, developmental transformation, environmental change, and intervals of greatly reduced activity. What sustains that continuity varies with the organism and the conditions. Regulation, repair, compensation, and reorganisation can each contribute by maintaining or re-establishing viable conditions. Preserved structures and environmental conditions can also carry part of the explanation.

Agency identifies the viability-oriented activity involved in such cases, while organised persistence identifies the continuity to be explained. Their explanatory relationship is established by showing how particular activities affect the conditions of continuity. Any additional gain from analysing the case under agency depends on what that analysis enables us to explain about the specified target, given the strongest relevant comparator.
