## What this figure shows

This figure represents temporal relationships within viability-oriented living organisation. Biological agency concerns viability-oriented activity in the present, while organised persistence concerns the continuity of viable organisation through change. Adaptation, inheritance, and transformation identify different ways in which living organisation may be reorganised, reconstituted, or changed across different temporal extents. Evolution concerns the historical transformation of persistence-sustaining organisation across generations.

The relationships among these concepts depend on the biological case concerned. Their arrangement in the figure should not be read as establishing a universal causal sequence, necessary temporal succession, or fixed dependency among them.

## APS temporal concepts

The figure distinguishes several temporal concepts relevant to APS explanation:

- **Agency** — viability-oriented activity in the present
- **Organised persistence** — continuity of viable organisation through change
- **Adaptation** — reorganisation under changing conditions
- **Inheritance** — reconstitution of viable organisation across generations
- **Transformation** — durable change within organised persistence
- **Evolution** — historical transformation of persistence-sustaining organisation across generations

These concepts identify distinguishable explanatory relationships rather than mandatory stages of a single biological sequence.

## Temporal architecture

APS does not treat these concepts as successive stages in a universal biological sequence. They distinguish temporal relationships through which living organisation can be analysed across present activity, ongoing continuity, reorganisation under changing conditions, generational continuity, durable transformation, and evolutionary history.

Their biological relationships are context-dependent. Inheritance, adaptation, transformation, and other processes may contribute differently to continuity and historical change in different systems. Evolution concerns the historical transformation of persistence-sustaining organisation across generations, but the causal and temporal dependencies involved must be established for the biological explanandum concerned.
