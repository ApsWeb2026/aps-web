Evolutionary organisation is not a single evolutionary mechanism.

Inheritance preserves continuity across generations. Variation generates alternative organisational possibilities. Selection influences which forms of organisation contribute to future continuity. Adaptation stabilises viable forms of persistence under particular conditions. Innovation expands the range of organisational possibilities available to life.

Each process contributes something different, but none alone explains the evolutionary organisation of life.

In APS, **evolutionary organisation** refers to the integrated continuity architecture formed by the interaction of these processes. Evolution therefore cannot be reduced to inheritance, selection, adaptation, or any other individual mechanism. It emerges from the coordinated organisation through which continuity is preserved, transformed, diversified, and extended across historical time.

Evolutionary organisation is therefore not a mechanism but an architecture: the historical organisation through which viability-oriented organised persistence remains continuous while continually generating novelty.
