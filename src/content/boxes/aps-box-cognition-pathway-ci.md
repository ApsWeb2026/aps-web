Within APS, the cognition pathway is an explanatory dependency architecture for distinguishing related organisational questions without treating one term as automatically producing the next.

The pathway can be summarised as:

Life  
↓  
Biological Agency  
↓  
Biological Evaluation  
↓  
Significance  
↓  
Integration  
↓  
Cognition  
↓  
Mind  
↓  
Selfhood  
↓  
Reflective Agency  
↓  
Meaning

The arrows express explanatory dependency rather than chronology, ontogeny, evolutionary succession, hierarchy, levels of organisation, increasing complexity, or automatic production of the next term. Earlier terms identify explanatory conditions relevant to later terms, but they do not by themselves establish that the later organisation is present.

The sequence does not describe separate substances, modules, components, or universally necessary stages through which organisms pass. It provides an APS research architecture for distinguishing related organisational questions while preserving the proposition-specific evidential status of each dependency.

Within this architecture, APS investigates how Biological Agency, Biological Evaluation, Significance, and Integration provide pre-cognitive explanatory dependencies; how cognition organises biological significance across time; and how Mind, Selfhood, Reflective Agency, and Meaning raise further organisational questions without being entailed by cognition alone.

The pathway therefore organises APS research questions without itself establishing that every proposed dependency has been biologically demonstrated.
