APS is an organised conceptual and methodological framework rather than a collection of independent terms and articles. Concepts such as Agency, Process, Scale, Viability, Organised Persistence, Biological Evaluation, Cognition, and Adaptation should therefore be interpreted in relation to their canonical definitions, documented distinctions, and roles within the wider framework.

Individual articles can be read independently for particular questions, but many also rely upon concepts and distinctions established elsewhere in APS. Cross-links, glossary entries, orientation pages, and pathway diagrams help readers identify these relationships without implying that every connection is a biological dependency or an established explanatory relation.

APS uses canonical definitions and controlled revision to make its conceptual commitments explicit and consistent across the corpus. These controls help readers identify what APS claims and how particular terms are being used. They do not establish that the corresponding biological claims are true or that APS provides explanatory gain over alternative accounts.

When reading APS, it is useful to ask:

- What does this concept mean in APS?
- What distinctions does its canonical definition preserve?
- How is it related to other APS concepts?
- Is a displayed relation conceptual, methodological, or a proposed biological dependency?
- What evidence or comparison would be required if an explanatory claim is being made?

New material should not be required merely to reinforce existing APS architecture. It should be assessed for its consequences for established definitions, distinctions, pathways, and claims. Where warranted, new evidence or analysis may preserve, qualify, retype, revise, or reject an existing formulation.

Understanding APS therefore involves more than accumulating definitions. It involves recognising how concepts and claims are organised across the framework while keeping conceptual coherence distinct from biological evidence and explanatory success.

APS proposes a way of organising biological questions around viability-oriented organisation and organised persistence. Where APS claims explanatory gain over another account, that gain requires target-matched comparison with the strongest relevant alternative.
