Living agents generate significance through biological evaluation.

Yet an entity need not itself evaluate in order to become an object of justified concern.

This distinction becomes important when considering species, ecosystems, rivers, landscapes, climate systems, and future generations. The central question is not whether such entities evaluate conditions for themselves, but what grounds their standing and how their claims should be weighed within environmental deliberation.
