A fundamental unit is not always the smallest component of a system.

The unit that matters depends on what needs explaining. Genes are central to many explanations of inheritance. Cells are central to explanations of how metabolic processes are coordinated. Organisms are central to explanations of how activities such as feeding, repair, and regulation work together to sustain a living system.

Genes, cells, organisms, species, and ecosystems can therefore all be biologically important in different ways. A unit may be fundamental to the structure of a system, central to a particular explanation, or significant in an evolutionary inquiry.

When APS asks what is fundamental to life, it asks what kind of organisation enables a living system to maintain itself through change.
