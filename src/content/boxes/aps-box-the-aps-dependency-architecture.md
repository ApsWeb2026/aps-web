The APS framework is organised through a coherent dependency architecture linking life, agency, evaluation, significance, integration, cognition, mind, selfhood, reflective agency, and meaning.

The pathway can be summarised as:

**Life → Agency → Biological Evaluation → Significance → Integration → Cognition → Mind → Selfhood → Reflective Agency → Meaning**

Each concept depends upon organisational conditions established by those that precede it, creating a progressively more sophisticated architecture of living organisation. The sequence does not describe a rigid developmental pathway, nor does it imply that later concepts are separate from earlier ones. Rather, it identifies how increasingly complex forms of organisation emerge from the challenge of maintaining viable continuity through time.

Life establishes the phenomenon requiring explanation. Agency reveals the active regulation of continuity, while biological evaluation emerges because conditions differ in their consequences for viability. Evaluation generates significance by distinguishing what matters for continued existence, and integration organises significance into coherent patterns of activity. Cognition extends these patterns across time through memory, anticipation, and adaptive responsiveness, while mind establishes a coherent relationship between an agent and its world. Selfhood stabilises that organisation through continual transformation, and reflective agency enables agents to evaluate and direct aspects of their own activity. Meaning emerges as organised significance becomes increasingly integrated, interpreted, and lived.

APS therefore progresses from life to meaning without abandoning its biological foundations. The later stages of the pathway remain continuous with the earlier ones because each emerges from organisational conditions established by the challenge of maintaining viable continuity through change.
