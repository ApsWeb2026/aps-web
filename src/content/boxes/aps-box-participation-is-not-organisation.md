Participating in a biological process is not the same as constituting a biological system.

Many entities participate in biological processes. Nutrients participate in metabolism. Environmental conditions participate in development. Viruses participate in evolution, inheritance, adaptation, and natural selection. Yet participation alone does not establish that an entity is itself alive.

In APS, living systems are defined by viability-oriented organisation. They actively maintain the conditions required for their own continued existence through ongoing self-maintaining activity. Their persistence is not merely a consequence of external processes but is produced through their own organised activity.

This distinction is particularly important when evaluating boundary cases such as viruses. Viruses participate extensively in biological and evolutionary processes, yet participation in those processes is not equivalent to exhibiting the viability-oriented organisation characteristic of living systems.

**APS perspective:** Participation in biological processes may be necessary for life, but it is not sufficient. Living systems are distinguished by organised persistence rather than by participation alone.
