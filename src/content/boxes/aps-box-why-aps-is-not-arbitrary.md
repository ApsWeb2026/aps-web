The Agency–Process–Scale (APS) framework does not begin by selecting a preferred set of biological concepts. Instead, it asks what explanatory dimensions must be present if living systems are to be understood as viability-oriented forms of organised persistence.

Agency, process, and scale are not introduced as convenient categories. They emerge from the explanatory requirements of biological organisation itself. Without agency, there is no way to explain how living systems regulate activity relative to viability conditions. Without process, continuity cannot be understood as something actively maintained through time. Without scale, the distributed relationships through which persistence is organised across spatial and temporal domains remain unexplained.

For this reason, APS does not claim that agency, process, and scale are the only useful biological concepts. It claims that any adequate account of living systems must address these explanatory dimensions, whether explicitly or under different terminology.

**Key Point.** APS is not an arbitrary framework imposed upon biology. It makes explicit the explanatory conditions required for biological intelligibility itself.
