Viruses are diagnostically important because they show why APS does not reduce life to replication, evolution, or structural organisation.

A virus outside a host is organised but not actively viability-oriented. It does not regulate, metabolise, or sustain the conditions of its own persistence. Inside a host, viral activity becomes biologically consequential, but only by exploiting the host’s metabolism, regulation, and organisational closure.

For APS, viruses are therefore best understood as **organisational parasites**. They participate in biological systems, perturb living organisation, and shape evolutionary and ecological dynamics, but they do not constitute autonomous viability-oriented systems in their own right.

This is why viruses sit at the boundary of life without dissolving that boundary.
