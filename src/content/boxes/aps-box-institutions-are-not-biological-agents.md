Institutions can organise decisions, preserve memory, allocate authority, and shape the consequences of collective action. Their reality lies in maintained roles, rules, procedures, infrastructures, records, and practices that coordinate social activity across time. These capacities make institutions organisationally real, but they do not establish Biological Agency, Biological Evaluation, or biological individuality.
