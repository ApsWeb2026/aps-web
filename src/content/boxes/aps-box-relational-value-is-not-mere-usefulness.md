Something can be valuable through the relations in which it participates without being a replaceable instrument.

A river, wetland, forest, or reef may be ecologically, historically, culturally, and generatively valuable even when its importance cannot be reduced to the immediate interests of any single organism.

Relational value is therefore neither mere usefulness nor arbitrary preference. It remains answerable to real organisation, dependency, history, vulnerability, and future possibility.
