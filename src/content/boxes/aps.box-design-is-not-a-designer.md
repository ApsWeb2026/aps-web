Design and designer are not the same concept.

Human artefacts are organised because they are intentionally designed. It is therefore natural to assume that any system exhibiting sophisticated organisation must also have a designer.

Living systems challenge this assumption.

Organisms exhibit extraordinary forms of organisation, including development, regulation, repair, adaptation, and reproduction. These features create a strong appearance of design. Yet modern biology provides no evidence that living systems are constructed through external planning or intention.

APS therefore distinguishes between the existence of design-like organisation and the existence of a designer.

The organisation observed in living systems is real. The question is not whether that organisation exists, but how it arises. APS interprets biological design as an expression of organised persistence: the continual generation, maintenance, repair, and reorganisation of the conditions required for continued existence.

Design-like organisation therefore does not imply a designer. It points instead to the distinctive organisational capacities of living systems themselves.
