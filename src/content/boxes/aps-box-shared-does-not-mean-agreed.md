Shared Evaluation does not require consensus, equal participation, or common judgment. Significance may become socially organised through cooperation, disagreement, conflict, persuasion, negotiation, resistance, or institutional imposition. What is shared is the evaluative field within which actions, expectations, and consequences become socially meaningful, not necessarily a single conclusion about what should be valued or done.
