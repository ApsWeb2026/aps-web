The cognition research stream brings the APS framework into dialogue with contemporary cognitive science, particularly the enactivist and embodied cognition traditions. If agency is grounded in the self-maintaining organisation of living systems, what does this imply for our understanding of cognition?

## Key Questions

- How did cognition become a biological problem?
- What distinguishes cognitive organisation from sophisticated biological regulation?
- How should proposed cases of cognition be compared with adequate non-cognitive explanations?
- How do embodied, ecological, enactive, distributed, and other contemporary approaches contribute to biological explanation of cognition?
- How are cognitive processes materially organised across spatial and temporal extents?

## Related Work

The historical and conceptual orientation is developed in [How Did Cognition Become a Biological Problem?](/articles/how-did-cognition-become-a-biological-problem/). The core APS account of cognitive organisation is developed in [The Cognitive Organisation of Life](/articles/the-cognitive-organisation-of-life/), while [Cognition — Where Does It Belong in Biology?](/articles/cognition-where-does-it-belong-in-biology/) situates cognition within the wider biological architecture. The cognition–regulation boundary is investigated through the APS work on minimal cognition.
